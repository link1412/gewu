# Pearl and enamel reference notes

Reference only. **Measured** means a specified specimen and method; **described** means an observation or catalogue account. Missing ranges below are gaps in the consulted sources, not proof that measurements do not exist.

**1. Pearl nacre at 10–60×**

- **Appearance, described:** overlapping, wavy growth boundaries can look locally parallel, then bend into contours or fingerprint-like spirals; avoid treating the whole pearl as a uniformly ruled surface. The Hope Pearl has a **25×** photograph (fig. 7); GIA documents fingerprint spirals on an unusual “hammered” pearl with a **500 µm field of view**. Neither supplies measured terrace spacing or relief height. [Hope Pearl, JoG 1994, p. 238](https://gem-a.com/images/Documents/JoG/Archive/1956-97/JoG1994_24_4.pdf); [GIA, Winter 2025](https://www.gia.edu/gems-gemology/winter-2025-microworld-fingerprint-pattern-hammered-pearls).
- **Dimensions—keep the measured features separate:**

  | Specimen / method | Reported dimensions | What they establish |
  |---|---|---|
  | Sectioned *Pinctada maxima* cultured pearls; calibrated electron microscopy | Aragonite platelet thickness **0.30–0.60 µm**, mean about **0.40 µm** | Internal layer thickness; **not a measurement of exposed terrace step height**. [Snow et al., 2004, pp. 1354–55](https://www.ruf.rice.edu/~rau/phys600/p1353.pdf) |
  | *P. margaritifera* shell; SEM at about 1700–1800× | Parallel groove spacing **3.38 µm** (296/mm) on the polished, strongly iridescent surface; average **11.5 µm** on the rougher inner surface; lamella thickness about **0.4 µm** | Shell-specific surface spacing and layer thickness; **not whole-pearl terrace or circled-groove dimensions**. [Liu et al., 1999](https://doi.org/10.1364/OE.4.000177) |

  **No defensible typical pearl-surface terrace spacing or direct step-height range was established here.** Equating a visible step to one platelet thickness would be a modelling assumption; steps can expose multiple layers.
- **Circled pearls:** a study sliced and optically examined **34 Ming cultured pearls, 11–19 mm long**. Their coaxial ring furrows are a separate scale of structure. In paired-ring specimens, grooves were deepest at an organic spot defect and became shallower around the circumference. The paper supplies **no typical groove width/depth range in µm**; its proposed formation mechanism is specific to this pearl type. [Gauthier et al., JoG 2018, pp. 240–250](https://gem-a.com/images/JoG/JOG_Autumn_2018.pdf).
- **Blemishes, described:** pinpoints/pits, dimples, bumps/welts, wrinkles, discoloured spots and dull patches; scratches, cracks and chips also occur. These are classification terms, not measured dimensions or frequency statistics. [CIBJO/DANAT guide, p. 26](https://cibjo.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/CIBJO-Guide-for-Classifying-Natural-Pearls-and-Cultured-Pearls.pdf).
- **Orient and BRDF:** GIA defines orient as multicolour iridescence and describes multilayer interference, while also discussing surface-grating diffraction. These mechanisms need not be exclusive. Liu's **514.5 nm laser diffraction experiment measured a shell**, supporting diffraction for that specimen; extending it to all pearl orient exceeds that experiment. GIA explicitly cautions against assuming identical shell/pearl optical causes. [GIA review, 2025](https://www.gia.edu/gems-gemology/summer-2025-phenomenal-gemstones). A **measured BRDF** (angular distribution of reflected light) appears in Tobitani et al., fig. 8, for a **pearl-oyster shell underside**; their pearl shader uses a simulated multilayer BRDF. No measured whole-pearl BRDF dataset was located. [Primary paper, 2013](https://ist.ksc.kwansei.ac.jp/~nagata/data/A_simulation_of_multilayer_thin-film_interference_for_pearl_material_preproduction.pdf).

**2. Chinese filigree, openwork and enamel**

- **花丝 / 累丝 + 烧蓝 / 点蓝:** draw fine gold/silver wire, twist, bend, weave or layer it, then solder the elements. 点蓝 places enamel paste; 烧蓝 fires it. This combination does not by itself establish transparent, unsupported enamel cells. The Palace Museum's **银烧蓝累丝圆盒**, **middle Qing**, has a thick silver framework, two finer wire sizes and transparent blue/green enamel. Its recorded **95 mm mouth diameter and 39 mm height** describe the object; no wire or cell measurements are supplied. [Palace Museum, 故00137786](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/tinware/229548.html).
- **Gauge / cell size:** practitioner Jeanette Caines reports twisted filigree wire “often 26-gauge” (gauge system unspecified), flattened to about **0.15 mm thickness**. This is a workshop account, not metrology of historical Chinese enamel or a universal diameter. **No museum-supported typical historical cell-span range was found**, nor a common gauge covering filigree, cloisonné and plique-à-jour. [Caines, 2019, p. 92](https://www.santafesymposium.org/s/2019-Caines_Jeanette_Ancient_Chinese_Filigree.pdf).
- **Cloisonné and 镂空:** attach bent copper/bronze partitions to a metal body, fill with enamel, fire repeatedly as it shrinks, then grind/polish and gild. A pierced rim can have enamelled metal members surrounding **empty openings**. Light passes through those openings; the metal backing prevents transmission through its enamelled areas. [Met technique account](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/chinese-cloisonne).
- **Plique-à-jour / 空窗珐琅:** enamel spans cells with no final backing, producing transmitted colour like miniature stained glass. In Japanese **shōtai-shippō / shōtai-jippō**, a temporary copper body can be dissolved with acid after enamelling. Backlighting makes the glass glow while metal partitions remain opaque (the latter is optical inference). The Japanese process/example establishes technique, not Chinese provenance. [V&A](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/metalworking-techniques); [Ashmolean, EA1990.5](https://jameelcentre.ashmolean.org/collection/7/10227/10243/all/per_page/50/offset/0/sort_by/seqn./start/1860/end/1950/object/12153).

**Documented Chinese openwork references** (museum dates are attributions, not necessarily inscribed years):

- **Met 29.110.49**, China, **19th century**, vase with four explicitly described openwork dragon cartouches; cloisonné, gilt bronze and champlevé. Recorded height **470 mm** is an overall dimension, not panel/wire size. [Museum record](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/40706).
- **Shenyang Palace Museum, 掐丝珐琅盘龙香亭**, **Qianlong reign**: pierced enamel ruyi elements beneath the eaves and an openwork column combining enamel clouds with a gilt dragon. No wire/cell dimensions supplied. [Museum record](https://www.sypm.org.cn/product_1/1161609286960390144.html).
- **Dish rim specifically:** Owen Jones's **1867**, *Examples of Chinese Ornament*, **plate LXIII (63)**, documents a cloisonné dish rim and identifies black areas as piercing. This dates the publication; it does **not** establish manufacture date or a current museum accession for that dish. [Original plate and description](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Owen_Jones_-_Examples_of_Chinese_Ornament_-_1867_-_plate_063.png).

**缠枝莲 composition:** the Palace Museum describes a stem/vine framework with intertwined branches and leaves forming a repeating band or all-over repeat. Early-Ming cloisonné studies identify repeated, symmetrical lotus units. For a procedural drawing, a defensible **inferred construction order** is: lay a continuous curling main stem; attach subsidiary curling branches and leaves; place flower heads on short connecting stalks. Use nested petals around a centre for a front-facing head, and a cupped/fan-shaped overlapping-petal profile for a side-facing head. This is a graphic reconstruction, not a documented universal workshop sequence or a requirement to alternate views. [Palace Museum definition](https://www.dpm.org.cn/lemmas/239382.html); [primary study of 25 early-Ming objects](https://jogg.cug.edu.cn/cn/article/doi/10.15964/j.cnki.027jgg.2026.04.009); [Met early-15th-century lotus-scroll dish, 1993.338](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/40766).
