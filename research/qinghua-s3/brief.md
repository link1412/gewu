# Research brief: blue-and-white porcelain (青花) as a physically based material

We render real materials from physics (spectral optics, measured parameters) and validate each against real photographs at a known scale. Next material: blue-and-white porcelain, especially the bright, clean look of new (modern Jingdezhen) pieces; Kangxi-period 翠毛蓝 is the historical bright reference.

## Rules (binding)

- Do not invent citations. Every fact needs a source: author, year, title, journal, and a DOI or URL. Tag each number: [M] measured in that source, [D] derived or read off a figure, [?] uncertain. If you cannot find something, write "not found". Do not fill the gap with a guess.
- You cannot download files. List URLs instead.
- Budget: about 40 minutes.

## Questions
1. **Cobalt blue's optics.**
   - Measured absorption spectra of Co2+ in silicate glass or glaze, 380-780 nm, ideally absolute: absorption coefficient per mm per wt% CoO.
   - The tetrahedral Co2+ bands (about 530, 590 and 640 nm): their positions and widths.
   - Any measured reflectance spectra (UV-Vis, FORS) of the blue areas on Ming or Qing blue-and-white, with the cobalt content if it is given.
2. **The colour's structure in cross-section.**
   - Where the pigment sits, at the body-glaze interface and diffusing into the glaze.
   - The thickness of the coloured zone, in micrometres.
   - Crystals in that zone (anorthite, cobalt-iron spinel, and so on).
   - Bubbles near the pigment.
   - "Heaping and piling" (iron-rich crystals) in Yongle and Xuande wares (苏麻离青).
   - SEM or EPMA cross-section sources.
3. **Ore and colour.**
   - Composition ranges (CoO, MnO, Fe2O3 and their ratios) for: 苏麻离青 (low Mn, high Fe); 国产青料 or 珠明料 (high Mn); 浙料 (Kangxi); 回青 (Jiajing, purple-blue); and modern refined cobalt oxide.
   - How each ingredient shifts the colour: Mn towards purplish grey, Fe towards blackish spots.
   - Peer-reviewed sources.
4. **The rest of the stack, with numbers.**
   - Jingdezhen porcelain body: reflectance spectrum, whiteness, translucency.
   - Transparent glaze: composition and refractive index; thickness on blue-and-white wares; bubble size and density; gloss (GU), for modern and for Kangxi wares.
   - The glaze's faint blue-green tint (Fe2+).
5. **Painting technique.**
   - 勾线 (outlining) and 分水 (graded washes): how many tones the washes take.
   - Typical brush-stroke widths.
   - How the concentration varies within a stroke: edges, pooling.
   - Typical motifs (缠枝莲, 云龙, ...).
   - Museum or academic descriptions.
6. **Reference images** (open-access, CC0 or CC BY, high resolution, with the object's physical size). For each, give: the image file's URL (largest size), the object page, the dimensions, the licence, and the date or period.
   - (a) Kangxi pieces with vivid blue. Places to look: Met, Cleveland, Smithsonian NMAA (Freer|Sackler), Rijksmuseum (CC0, very large images), Art Institute of Chicago, National Palace Museum Taipei open data.
   - (b) Modern Jingdezhen blue-and-white under a CC licence (Wikimedia Commons).
   - (c) Close-ups showing brush strokes and bubbles at 20 px/mm or more.

## Output
- report: a structured report with numeric tables, sources and tags.
- download.txt: one image URL per line, then a tab, then the object page, size and licence.
