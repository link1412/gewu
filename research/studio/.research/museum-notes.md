# Museum/object photography and modifier sizes — research notes

These are primary-source accounts of photography, not exhibition lighting. **Reported setup** means an institution/photographer states what was used; it is observational documentation, not a radiometric measurement. **Guidance/estimate** means a prescribed starting point, not a measured constant. **Manufacturer spec** means a nominal product dimension. Do not label museum guidance as measured data. None of the sources establishes a universal museum catalogue lighting geometry.

## Institution-specific findings

| Institution | Published evidence and classification | Precise source location |
|---|---|---|
| Metropolitan Museum of Art | Its handling manual specifies electronic flash/strobe for three-dimensional objects of every material; HMI and LED for works on paper, fluorescent light bars for copy work. **Institutional guidance**, not a measured SPD or geometry. It supplies no standard softbox size, position, angle or background. | Barbara Bridgers, “Guidelines for Photography of Artworks,” in *The Care and Handling of Art Objects: Practices in the Metropolitan Museum of Art* (2019), **printed p.178**, “Photographic Lighting.” [Official PDF](https://resources.metmuseum.org/resources/metpublications/pdf/The_Care_and_Handling_of_Art_Objects_Practices_in_The_Metropolitan_Museum_of_Art_2019.pdf#page=179). |
| Palace Museum, Beijing | **Reported setup:** **two** flash heads, each with a **60 × 60 cm** softbox, front left/right at **45°**; grey photographic background paper covers a glass turntable. Purpose: consistent soft, low-contrast surface-texture acquisition and stitching into publication/web rollout images. For immovable sculpture the camera and both lights move around the object to preserve lighting. This is a documented special workflow, not a claim that all catalogue shots use it. | Jin Yueping, “浅谈‘数字文物’的表面纹理采集及影像后期技术 / Capturing Surface Texture of the Digital Collections and Imaging Technologies,” *故宫学刊* (2019), **printed p.554**, §一(一)2(1–2), **Fig.2**, and §一(二)2. | Palace Museum, Beijing (alternative artistic documentation) | **Author guidance/estimate:** at least **four** lamps—top, key, fill and background—with power and direction chosen for the object. Diffuse light through tracing paper/white acrylic or bounce with white paper/reflectors. Background treatment can be a smooth hanging sweep; pale neutral backgrounds are discussed, with a warning that saturated colours contaminate glossy objects. Thus its published practice includes materially different lighting objectives. | Meng Chengguang, “博物馆之文物摄影 / Photography of Cultural Relics for Museums,” *故宫学刊* (2019), **printed pp.581–582**, §二2 “灯光的运用” and §二3 “背景的处理.” [Official PDF](https://img.dpm.org.cn/Uploads/File/2020/11/13/u5fae2a6a1aea0.pdf#page=4). |

Exact angles/distances are not stated. Model names identify equipment, not measured electrical/optical output. This directly establishes a real collection-object shoot. | [Official image record](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/image/1613862566), **Description**. | Getty Museum | A first-party account of photographing a porcelain vase says the rollout required unusually flat, clean illumination to avoid shadows/reflections, explicitly contrasting this with the normal way they photograph three-dimensional objects. **Reported method**, no numeric light geometry, lamp type, size or background specified. ## Useful numerical museum guidance (additional institutions)

- **US National Park Service — guidance/estimate:** key at **6 ft**, fill at **7–8 ft**; key **45°**, fill **60–70°**, both measured from the object's front plane; lamps high enough to light the top and front. These are explicitly starting standards to adjust for the object. *Museum Handbook*, Part II (2000), Appendix K, **printed p.K-8, Figs.K.4–K.6**, [official PDF](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/museums/upload/MHII_AppK_Photography-2.pdf). The PDF's indexed text is available, but direct URL returned 404 during verification; treat this as a historical source with a stale direct link.
- **Canadian Conservation Institute — guidance/estimate:** normal lighting for preservation imaging and catalogue beauty shots puts lights on both sides at **45°**; this is specifically illustrated with an embroidered artwork, so avoid inferring the same geometry is ideal for every sculpture. [Official video transcript](https://www.canada.ca/en/conservation-institute/services/learning-activities/lighting-techniques.html), **“Normal light” paragraph**.
- **National Museums Scotland — guidance/estimate:** **two** equally powerful lights, equidistant at **45°**, yield flat illumination for paper/books; **one** low raking light with a reflector reveals carved/inscribed/coin texture better. This supports separate flat-reproduction and shape/texture lighting presets. [Official guidance](https://www.nms.ac.uk/our-impact/national-work/training-and-guidance-for-museums/photographing-collections/lighting-and-colour), **“Picking out fine detail and texture” / “Two lights.”**

## FADGI / Metamorfoze scope

- The **2023 FADGI third edition** has material-specific evaluation tables for documents, photographs/film, paintings and other two-dimensional art; **chapter 3, contents p.6, §§3.1–3.15**, contains no table giving a sculpture/catalogue lighting arrangement. It should not be cited as a source of a universal 3D studio geometry. The separate FADGI web content-category framework does include **Category 3D**, but concerns choice of image sets and views, not numerical lamps/modifiers. [Official technical guidelines PDF](https://www.digitizationguidelines.gov/guidelines/FADGITechnicalGuidelinesforDigitizingCulturalHeritageMaterials_ThirdEdition_05092023.pdf#page=6); [Category 3D.1](https://www.digitizationguidelines.gov/guidelines/ccdo-subcat-3D1.html).
- **Metamorfoze Image Quality v2.0, April 2025**, describes the programme as preservation of **paper heritage**, **p.5 “Metamorfoze”**. No 3D object lighting prescription found. Do not turn target-uniformity/image-quality criteria into a sculpture lighting recipe. [Official PDF](https://www.metamorfoze.nl/sites/default/files/documents/Preservation%20Imaging%20Guidelines%20English%202.0%2C%20April%202025.pdf#page=5) (indexed text available; direct fetch 403).

## Manufacturer modifier dimensions

All entries below are **manufacturer specs**, nominal dimensions, not independent measurements or a frequency survey of what museums most often use. Octa values are diameter. Rectangular/strip values are width × length. These give realistic purchasable scale choices without inventing a “usual” statistical size.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Elinchrom Rotalux | **60 × 80**, **90 × 110 cm** rectangles; **70 × 70**, **100 × 100 cm** squares | **35 × 100**, **50 × 130 cm** | **100**, **135**, **175 cm**; Deep Octa **70**, **100 cm** | [Official Rotalux page](https://dev.elinchrom.com/ranges/rotalux-softbox/), **Specifications tables**, rows **Size / Diameter**, headings Square, Strip/Recta, Octa/Deep Octa. Avoid the webpage's apparently erroneous strip article-number row; the dimensions/headings agree with its product chart. |
| broncolor | **35 × 60**, **60 × 60**, **60 × 100**, **100 × 100**, **90 × 120**, **120 × 180 cm** | **30 × 120**, **30 × 180 cm** | **75**, **150 cm** | [Official System Catalogue (2015)](https://broncolor.swiss/assets/img/Support/Downloads/System-Catalogue/Systemcatalogue_2015_EN.pdf#page=42), **printed p.42**, **“Softboxes, Octaboxes and Speedrings”**, tables **Softbox / Octabox**. These agree with the indexed 2026 catalogue p.74 (the newer PDF direct link returned 404). No numerical source-backed ratios of light power, diffuser transmission, angular emission profiles or full lamp-to-subject distances were found for the five requested museums. Treat missing renderer inputs as explicit artist choices/estimates, not as museum measurements.
