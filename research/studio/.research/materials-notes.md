# Backdrop and grey-card research notes

All reflectances below are linear fractions of incident light unless a percentage sign appears. A single quoted reflectance does not characterize a velvet BRDF. Values are specimen- or product-specific; no RGB/HEX swatch was treated as measured albedo.

## Black velvet cloth

- **Published estimate, not measured data:** a useful explicitly low-confidence scalar is **0.01**. IIIT Allahabad's own *Lecture Series on Image Processing*, slide **20** (PDF page 20), “Image Model,” lists black velvet under “Typical values.” It gives neither wavelength, geometry, textile identification nor experimental provenance. This can be an initial renderer estimate only, not a validated studio velvet specification. [Lecture PDF](https://silp.iiita.ac.in/wp-content/uploads/cv_Image_Processing_2.pdf#page=20).
- **Measured cloth, angular data exist:** Hsia & Richmond, *Bidirectional Reflectometry. Part I* (NBS, 1976), **Figure 6.6, printed p. 202**, explicitly measures “black velvet cloth,” with the incident beam polarized normal to the plane of incidence. The figure includes directional–hemispherical reflectance, while the paper studies angular reflection with a laser. Its published graph was located, but no numerical wavelength curve or reliable table was recovered. [NIST original PDF](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/jres/80A/jresv80An2p189_A1b.pdf#page=14), [primary publication record and figure captions](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32196293/).
- **Measured visible-light BRDF, not a spectral curve:** Filip & Vávra's *How Dark is Dark?* **§3.2, Figure 4 (albedo), Figure 6 (total hemispherical reflectance)** has a genuine velvet-cloth specimen alongside Musou fabric and paints. RGB measurements are relative units and are angularly dependent; do not convert an unlabeled graph bar into a wavelength-independent material constant. No tabulated reflectance values were recovered. It is a preprint; §Data availability says supplementary BRDF measurements/rendering code exist, without an accessible supplementary link in the arXiv version. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.05094v1).

**Avoid a category error:** Nextel “Black Velvet” and Acktar “Metal Velvet” are coatings, not conventional velvet fabric.

## Photographic seamless paper

- **Colorama Granite: manufacturer nominal specification = 18% grey.** The Colorama-branded brochure, PDF **page 2**, footnote **††**, says “Granite is suitable for use when 18% grey is required.” This gives no measured spectrum, geometry or tolerance; paper production and surface lighting can change appearance according to the adjacent footnote. It does not establish that another grey paper is 18%. [Colorama brochure, hosted by Daylight Studios](https://daylightstudios.com/wp-content/uploads/colorama-muestra-2015_RGB-colours.pdf#page=2).
- **Savage white/grey/black:** root agent is independently processing Robin Myers' measured Savage spectral library. Do not use Savage's display RGB/HEX/CMYK swatch chart as reflectance. [First-person spectral library](https://www.chromaxion.com/spectral-library.php), **Reflective → Paper → Savage / Superior Seamless**. Myers explicitly says spectra represent specimens in his collection, rather than definitive product specifications; attribution and noncommercial redistribution conditions are on this page.

## Kodak grey cards

- **Manufacturer nominal specification:** the **Gray Card Plus** grey, black, white patches are **18%, 3%, 90%**, respectively. [Kodak](https://www.kodak.com/en/motion/page/gray-card/), **opening paragraph**. This is a product nominal value, not an individually calibrated card.
- **Manufacturer spectral graph:** *How to Use KODAK Gray Card*, PDF **page 3**, “Spectral Reflectance,” plots grey and white sides over **400–700 nm**. It is graphical, without tabulated spectral samples; no exact spectral CSV was fabricated. [Kodak-authored instruction PDF at ACES](https://docs.acescentral.com/assets/pdfs/kodak-grey-card.pdf#page=3).
- **Corroborating published specification:** EBU **Tech 3335, Annex 1, printed p. 33, Figure 17** reproduces Kodak's graphs and describes **18% / 90%**, with **±2%** tolerance over **400–700 nm**. It does not clarify whether that tolerance is relative or percentage points; preserve the source's wording. This is a quoted manufacturer specification, not an EBU independent measurement. [EBU PDF](https://tech.ebu.ch/docs/tech/tech3335.pdf#page=33).
- **Independent measured counterexample, not a modern-card replacement:** Palmer's *Field Standards of Reflectance* measured older Kodak Neutral Test Cards relative to Halon. **Table 2, printed p. 1624**, grey-side band reflectance factors **0.315 / 0.242 / 0.224**, with **Table 1** actual instrument passbands **418–489 / 507–580 / 597–690 nm**, respectively. These are broadband instrument results, not monochromatic samples; do not create a wavelength CSV by assigning them to band centers. **Table 4** averages four cards and bands 2–5 to grey **0.250**, white **0.940**, ratio **3.76**. **p. 1623** states a **0°/30°** geometry; **p. 1624** warns of band-1 anomalies, non-Lambertian orientation dependence, and the need to characterize individual cards for exacting work. [Primary paper, Tables 1–4](https://www.asprs.org/wp-content/uploads/pers/1982journal/oct/1982_oct_1623-1625.pdf#page=2).

Renderer implication: a flat nominal 18% grey card is a documented simplification; spectral accuracy requires an identified measured card specimen, not confidence inferred from the nominal name.
