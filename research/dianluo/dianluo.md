# 点螺 and the optics of nacre — film research

Research date: 2026-10-28. [I] This is a bounded research pass; **not found** means not recovered and verified in this pass, rather than proof that a datum does not exist. [I]

Tags: **[M]** measured in the cited source, including explicitly identified fits and museum dimensions; **[D]** figure reading or secondary-source information; **[A]** abstract only; **[I]** inference, calculation, modelling assumption, or research status. [I] Documentary descriptions and technical specifications are marked [D] to avoid calling them measurements; their primary/secondary status is separately identified. [I]

The useful direction is to keep specimen-dependent stack statistics separate from craft-sheet thickness, surface-grating geometry and lacquer finishing. [I] The decisive evidence is that SEM “tablet thickness” can include the organic gap, grating periods are micrometres rather than tablet nanometres, and documented 罩漆 does not determine the final surviving film. ### Organic layers, anisotropy and dispersion

S1 uses **25nm organic thickness, n=1.43**, and scalar aragonite **n=1.63**, with no visible dispersion. [I: source model assumptions] These are usable reproduction settings, not new measurements of organic layers in Qing inlay shells. [I] S3 instead uses **norganic=1.3** and naragonite=1.6; its ± thicknesses are modelling inputs. [D] Their disagreement is a parameter uncertainty, not a measured species conversion. [I]

At589nm, the index ordering is **nα=1.530, nβ=1.681, nγ=1.686**; crystal-axis ordering is **nc=nα, na=nβ, nb=nγ**, rather than alphabetical matching of α,β,γ to a,b,c. [D: S1/S4] S4 reports handbook intervals α1.530–1.531, β1.680–1.681, γ1.685–1.686. [D] Aragonite **400–700nm dispersion coefficients or tabulated principal-index curves: not found**. [I] No calcite Sellmeier fit is silently substituted. [I]

S1 describes c-axis orientation around the layer normal with deviations up to±30° and variable in-plane a/b orientations. [D] This is crystal-orientation spread, **not** a measurement of layer tilt relative to a polished craft surface. [I] An anisotropic calculation must rotate the dielectric tensor; nα alone is not the index of both transverse polarisations at normal incidence. [I]

## 2. Layer tilt, exposed edges, grating and “flames”

Both proposed papers **exist and were read beyond their abstracts**: S5 is1999,4(5),177–182; S3 is2004,12(20),4847–4854. [D]

S3's narrow-groove diffraction values are **2.9,4.5,5.3,4.9,4.1µm** (Table1); coarse-groove values are **62,43,42,63,51µm** (Table2). [M] Its reported means are4.3±0.9µm and52±10µm; SEM means4.6µm and43µm. [M] The two groove directions are approximately perpendicular; do not assign both periods to one parallel stack. [M]/[I] The laser wavelength is632.8nm, incidence approximately40°, spot approximately3mm. [M]

S5 observes polished *P. margaritifera* with3.38µm smooth fine grooves, compared with11.5µm rougher grooves on its inner surface. [M] Its514.5nm argon-ion laser experiment confirms diffraction in this particular polished specimen. [M] Its broad inference that nacre interference cannot contribute should not override S1's later interference spectroscopy or S3's coexistence interpretation. [I]

**Measured layer-to-polished-surface tilt angle: not found.** [I] If adjacent surface grooves are intersections of planes separated normally by h, their surface period is p=h/sinφ. [I] Using S5's h≈0.4µm and p3.38µm givesφ≈6.8°; using S3's composite h≈0.5µm and p4.3µm givesφ≈6.7°. [I] These are conditional geometric estimates, not reported measurements, and cease to apply if the grooves arise from another structure or span several layers. [I]

**Lateral colour-patch / “flame” size distribution: not found.** [I] The3mm laser spot,5×5mm cut sample and micrometre groove widths are measurement/surface scales, not measured flame diameters. [I] For the film, a layer-normal field and a grating-direction field should be treated as separate uncalibrated spatial quantities until a polished inlay sample is measured. [I]
