# Met gold-pink bowl photography and enamel colour: evidence report

Research date: **2 November 2026**. Target: **1971.180.248**, Met object **46397**.

## Findings and recommended interpretation

**The strongest period source is a 2011 paper by the Met Photograph Studio itself. It documents calibrated digital capture, but does not identify this bowl's camera, lighting, polarizers, flags, or processing settings.** None of the three proposed explanations can be established for this exposure from the public evidence located.

Recommended direction: treat **lighting geometry and photographic tone response as separate unknowns**, using a documented studio reconstruction as the comparison method. Do not assign Capture One's Film Standard curve, cross-polarization, or black cards to this photograph without capture-specific evidence. The later Met reference-asset paper below is unusually relevant to this exact render-versus-photograph problem.

| Requested result | Outcome |
|---|---|

### Why a target on another object would only partly solve the problem

**Methodological inference:** a chart from another session could characterize that session's delivered image, but would not establish the bowl's curve without evidence of shared camera/profile/exposure/processing settings. A single gray patch constrains exposure under assumptions; several known neutral patches are needed to constrain a response curve. Neutral patches alone do not establish the saturated-red colour transform or recover clipped data.

## 3. Measured colour and spectra

### Closest actual historic Qing reflectance work: measurements announced, values absent

Agnieszka Gawin and Rosa Seepma, **“Searching for White Gold,” 7 April 2025**. [Firsthand Leiden research account](https://www.leidenarchaeologyblog.nl/articles/searching-for-white-gold), “Second day of analysis” and “The equipment.”

Exact fragments: **“Chinese ruby-backs”**; **“the FORS provides a UV-Visible light reflectance spectrum of the different coloured enamel decorations.”**

The account describes pXRF and FORS measurements at Keramiekmuseum Princessehof in **December 2024**, including Qing Chinese wares. It is a strong lead because reflectance, rather than only elemental analysis, was actually measured. **Inspection outcome:** the located post gives no accession numbers, spectra, numerical reflectance, L\*a\*b\*, white-standard specification, or specular/measurement geometry. It is not a usable colour dataset. No researcher was contacted.

### Historic Qing ruby-back study: do not mistake luminescence for reflectance

Philippe Colomban, Hui Tang, and Gulsu Simsek-Franci, **“Non-Invasive Study of Gold Nanoparticles in Famille rose and Ruby-Back Qing Porcelain by Luminescence, Low-Wavenumber Raman Scattering and pXRF,” Applied Sciences 15 (2025), 12265**. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/app152212265), [local PDF](papers/Colomban_2025_Gold_Nanoparticles_Famille_Rose.pdf).

Exact quote, **p. 11, Fig. 6**: **“Fluorescence maxima of the ruby glazes are generally centered near 545 nm”**. Section 2.3 specifies **“457.9 and 514.5 nm”** laser excitation.

This is optical evidence from historic-attributed Qing/famille-rose wares, but the ~545 nm quantity is an **emission** maximum under laser excitation. The inspected paper does not provide the requested visible reflectance-factor curve or L\*a\*b\* dataset. **Inference:** using this peak as a green reflectance maximum in a material model would confuse two different measurements.

### Modern famille-rose pigment: actual colour/spectral analysis, incomplete accessible data

曹春娥等 / Cao Chun'e et al., **“无铅、砷金红粉彩颜料的呈色机理 / Colouration Mechanism of Lead and Arsenic Free Gold Ruby Famille-Rose Pigments,” Journal of the Chinese Ceramic Society 39(11) (2011), 1734–1739**. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.14062/j.issn.0454-5648.2011.11.022), [primary publisher abstract](https://jccsoc.cbpt.cnki.net/portal/journal/portal/client/paper/9293f260e984b5d4e83ebd667bac01f7).

Exact fragments: **“spectrophotometer”**; **“20–30 nm”**; **“strong absorption of green light of about 520 nm wavelength”**.

The abstract describes measurements on **modern prepared lead/arsenic-free gold-ruby famille-rose pigment**, not historic Qing enamel. It reports a gold-particle size range and absorption feature, but the accessible primary abstract supplies neither full L\*a\*b\* values nor calibrated reflectance magnitudes. No authorized open full PDF was found. An absorption-band wavelength alone cannot determine absolute colour.

### Directly relevant theoretical paper: simulated, not measured, spectra

Gen Li, Cui Jia, Baoqiang Kang, He Li, and Yong Lei, **“Simulated reflection spectra of copper red glaze and gold ruby-pink enamel,” EPJ Plus 140 (2025), 368**, published **7 May 2025**. [Publisher](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1140/epjp/s13360-025-06287-z).

## 4. What the evidence says about the three hypotheses

The **3.5×** side/front ratio and the **2012-07-31/sRGB** metadata are supplied by the brief; the precise sampling method and original file were not available. They are not independently verified measurements in this report.

| Hypothesis | Supported statement | What remains unproven |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Photographic tone mapping darkened the front | Capture One documents a shadow-deepening rendering mechanism; the period Met paper discusses response-curve ambiguity. | The bowl's converter, curve, local edits, and scene-to-JPEG mapping. An sRGB profile alone does not encode that history. |
| (b) Polarizer / cross-polarization | No primary historical Met ceramics disclosure was located. Different views have different visible reflection strength. | Presence, type, source polarizers, analyzer angle, and consistency between exposures. A lens polarizer and fully crossed source/camera polarization are not interchangeable assumptions. |
| (c) Black cards / flags | Later Met studio reconstruction documents cards and modifiers. | Any black card or flag in the older bowl exposure. A dark reflected environment could arise without a deliberately placed black card. |

These are **inferences and evidence limits**, not a diagnosis of the original capture.

### Small direct check on the current JPEG

**Own measurement:** in O1, a 100 × 100 pixel central-front patch, **x=[950,1050), y=[650,750)** with origin at the upper-left, has median **RGB=(74,24,24)**, channel minima **(58,18,16)**, and **zero pixels with a zero channel**. This selected patch is dark without hard clipping to digital black. It does **not** exclude shadow compression elsewhere or a steep tone curve. [Annotated locations](evidence/bowl_roi_preview.jpg), [patch measurements](evidence/bowl_roi_samples.json).

The stored auxiliary luminances assume sRGB because the current files are untagged. They are **display-relative linear values**, not recovered scene radiance or glaze reflectance. The side patches are illustrative; they do not reproduce the brief's unspecified 3.5× measurement.

### Practical next discrimination, based on the evidence

1. **Match encoding before comparing ratios.** The [W3C sRGB relative-luminance definition](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/relative-luminance.html) specifies **“L=0.2126×R+0.7152×G+0.0722×B”**, with inverse sRGB transfer applied to each channel first. For a normalized channel c, use c/12.92 below 0.04045 and ((c+0.055)/1.055)^2.4 above it. This removes display encoding; it does not undo photographic rendering or establish reflectance.
2. **Test physical set geometry with the material fixed.** The primary [PBRT optics treatment](https://pbr-book.org/4ed/Reflection_Models/Specular_Reflection_and_Transmission), opening paragraph, describes **“specular materials that only reflect light into a discrete set of directions.”** Inference: the front can reflect a dark camera-side environment while the curved sides reflect bright sources. Missing frontal glare is not itself a contradiction of dielectric optics. Follow the documented Met reconstruction method: source size/position, camera, backdrop, cards/scrims, and environment fill should be explicit variables.
3. **Separate tone mapping from reflection suppression.** Inference: compare a calibrated neutral-target render and a glossy neutral proxy under the same candidate lighting, then test a tone curve and a polarization-aware reflection model separately. A curve changes pixel values according to its mapping; polarization changes which optical components reach the camera. Multiple parameter combinations may match a single JPEG, so a visual match alone cannot establish the historical cause.
4. **Use same-session evidence to resolve the ambiguity.** The decisive missing materials are the bowl's uncropped chart exposure/raw or master TIFF, profile/curve settings, and set photograph or capture notes. For enamel colour, seek calibrated FORS/reflectance with geometry and white reference, plus illuminant/observer and specular inclusion for any L\*a\*b\*. No request for these has been sent.
