# Surface weathering research notes (item 3)

Research date 2026-10-29. Publisher URLs below remain usable. No local paper falsely claimed as downloaded.

## Most decisive evidence so far

**Do not drive all satin appearance from elapsed age.** Guan kiln archaeological samples already have firing-created bubbles/crystals/phase separation that explain a jade-like scattering body, and archaeological Longquan glazes from one shipwreck span shiny and heavily corroded states. Surface gloss and bulk opacity are separate controls. No controlled fresh/heirloom/excavated Guan experiment located.

### S1 — Actual gloss measurements, Southern Song Guan versus Longquan Ge

于欢 / Huan Yu; 陈猛 / Meng Chen; 周健儿 / Jianer Zhou; 董晓烽 / Xiaofeng Dong; 程家太 / Jiatai Cheng. **南宋官窑与龙泉哥窑釉面成分对比分析** (Comparative analysis of glaze composition of Southern Song Guan and Longquan Ge). *陶瓷学报 / Journal of Ceramics* **42(6)** (2021), **1072–1079**. DOI [10.13957/j.cnki.tcxb.2021.06.022](https://doi.org/10.13957/j.cnki.tcxb.2021.06.022). [Official journal abstract](https://qkzzs.jcu.edu.cn/info/1011/4315.htm); [publisher PDF](https://www.sciengine.com/doi/pdf/75869AEA265B403BACB28065DF5A359D).

- **Measured in this source, p.1074 Table 1:**

| Samples | Colour | n blocks | Mean gloss (table label: Gloss) | L* |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Southern Song Guan | 粉青 / pink-green | 2 | 46.70 | 51.85 |
| Longquan Ge | 粉青 / pink-green | 38 | 60.56 | 48.38 |
| Southern Song Guan | 米黄 / cream | 1 | 45.20 | 52.00 |
| Longquan Ge | 米黄 / cream | 6 | 64.03 | 38.70 |

- **Measured microstructure, authors' mechanism:** Jiaotanxia Guan has dense ~10 µm bubbles and small unmelted quartz; Longquan Dayao/Xikou Wayaoyang Ge bubbles are tens–hundreds µm and less dense. Authors attribute Guan's semi-matte jade quality to scattering by bubbles/quartz; Longquan's higher gloss to bubbles plus vitrification.
- **Limit:** Archaeological kiln-to-kiln comparison, not an ageing experiment. Longquan Ge here must not be silently equated with all heirloom Ge. Gloss angle, calibration, instrument and cleaning still need full-text verification. No Ra located.

### S2 — Jiaotanxia Guan microstructure (bulk scattering, not an ageing study)

Zhao Ren, Han Wang, Zongming Liu, Mengyi Zhang, Fen Wang, Jianfeng Zhu, Biao Zhang, Pei Shi. **The mechanisms of color, opacifying, and cracks in celadon glazes from the Southern Song Guan Kiln.** *npj Heritage Science* **14**, 187 (2026). DOI [10.1038/s40494-026-02383-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s40494-026-02383-0). [Article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s40494-026-02383-0?error=cookies_not_supported); [PDF](https://www.nature.com/articles/s40494-026-02383-0.pdf).

- **Measured:** Six excavated Jiaotanxia sherds; XRD crystals Fig.6, bubbles counted (200 each) Fig.7; SEM droplet estimates **21,28,27,22,30,22 nm**, G1–G6, Fig.8. No GU/Ra.
- **Authors' interpretation:** Thick high-viscosity glaze traps bubbles; crystals and separated domains contribute bulk light scattering/jade-like opacity. Not proof that original surface was rough or weathered.
- **Preparation caveat:** SEM samples etched **1 vol% HF,30s**; etched micrographs cannot stand for unaltered surface topography. XPS removes ~50 µm by sanding; that is preparation, **not a measured weathering thickness**. Color L*≈60±10 is not gloss.
- **Internal caution:** Text alternates between weak scattering from <30nm domains and significant Rayleigh scattering; do not rely on it for precise optical weights.

### S3 — Same shipwreck, bright-as-new versus lackluster Longquan

Yan He, Weidong Li, Jianan Li, Changsong Xu, Xiaoke Lu. **Research on the degradation of ancient Longquan celadons in the Dalian Island shipwreck.** *npj Materials Degradation* (2022). DOI [10.1038/s41529-022-00217-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41529-022-00217-5). [Article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41529-022-00217-5); [PDF](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41529-022-00217-5.pdf).

- **Direct observations:** Eleven Yuan wreck sherds: 01–05 bright-as-new,06–07 lackluster,08–10 matte,11 underfired (Methods, Supplementary Table3). Well-preserved transparent glazes have scratches/pits but no obvious corrosion; damaged surfaces show pits, porous aggregates, delamination (Figs1–3). Matte glazes corrode more severely (Figs8–10).
- **Authors' interpretation:** Physical abrasion plus chemical corrosion mutually promote rough/lackluster appearance; crystal-rich matte structure partly originates in composition/lower firing temperature, and is more corrosion susceptible. Thus a matte excavated surface can contain both original firing texture and later damage.
- **Limit:** No GU/Ra; not terrestrial Guan nor heirloom comparator. Shards desalinated in deionized water. Same chronology does not yield one surface state.

## Additional primary source: replication

Li Jiazhi, Deng Zequn, Xu Jiming. **Technical Studies and Replication of Guan Ware, an Ancient Chinese Ceramic.** *MRS Bulletin* **26(1)** (2001),31–37. DOI[10.1557/mrs2001.16](https://doi.org/10.1557/mrs2001.16). [Author-institution PDF](https://www.sic.cas.cn/zt/lijiazhi/lwzz/201905/P020190513376253648067.pdf).

- **Direct replica observations, pp.33–34, Figs5–7:** Fresh modern replicas had bubbles, quartz and anorthite microcrystals similar to archaeological Jiaotanxia glaze; authors attribute jade-like character to scattering. Higher firing remelts anorthite and increases transparency. No age required for this mechanism; no GU/Ra supplied.
- **Quoted/earlier work:** Archaeological firing ranges and body water absorption on p.33 are explicitly attributed to refs2,4,7, not newly measured in this paper.

## Explicit gaps and rendering implications (inferences)

- No controlled measurement series of same-composition Guan/Ge/Longquan: newly fired → heirloom → excavated. No Guan/Ge heirloom versus excavated GU comparison, no measured natural-surface Ra, and no calibrated age→roughness relation found.
- No primary evidence located for characteristic weathering iridescence of Guan/Ge; nearby examples are other ware/compositions, not transferable proof. Do not add rainbow ageing simply because an object is old.
- No primary quantitative decomposition of 酥油光 into original manufacture versus centuries of use found. Evidence supports original bulk scattering; burial can add real surface corrosion and dulling, contingent on composition and environment.
- Inference for shader: separate bulk-scattering controls from surface roughness/abrasion/pitting controls and stain controls. Shiny excavated surfaces remain plausible. A fixed age slider that automatically makes every800-year object matte is contradicted by Dalian sample variability.
