# Fresh crackle, staining, and delayed crazing: source notes

Research date: 2026-10-29. Primary literature, institutional repositories and museum object records. Original PDF downloads failed because shell outbound sockets are blocked; web PDF/text was inspected where accessible. Root is coordinating download status. Source extract records below are research summaries, not original PDFs.

## Recommended conclusion for report

Use transparent, unstained fracture geometry as the fresh starting point; do not bake yellow/brown pigment into every fresh crack. Keep intentional staining separate from exposure/burial: old does not imply dark crackle, and a dark line does not imply antiquity. Evidence for natural brown/yellow deposition in excavated Guan is stronger than evidence that tea caused any particular heirloom Guan/Ge crack. Carbon staining is demonstrated in modern Ge replication and supported analytically in one excavated Ming Ge-type specimen and in the Laohudong study. Different wares and histories must remain explicit. No optical measurements establishing a universal fresh white/silver/glitter appearance were located.

## F1 — fresh Guan and residue chemistry; original study with cited fresh-appearance claim

**Zihan Li, Feng Yuan, Jianwen Cao, Anke Hein. “Insights into the Residue Trapped in Glaze Cracks of Archaeological Ceramics Using Microchemical Analysis.” Microscopy and Microanalysis 28(6), 1878–1889 (2022). DOI [10.1017/S1431927622012375](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1431927622012375).** [Institutional record](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A4147a86e-094d-44e3-9d39-03c89b300e47); [author manuscript](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A4147a86e-094d-44e3-9d39-03c89b300e47/files/s2b88qd441); [author-uploaded proof text inspected](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/363317892_Insights_into_the_Residue_Trapped_in_Glaze_Cracks_of_Archaeological_Ceramics_Using_Microchemical_Analysis).

- **Quoted from previous literature, not a fresh-ware measurement in this paper:** Discussion / Coloring Mechanisms, proof p.8 (corresponds approximately final p.1885): fresh Guan fractures described as transparent and colorless, and many never-buried Palace Museum Guan as retaining transparent cracks. The paragraph cites Chen et al.1984, Wu et al.2009, Miao et al.2012.
- **Own measured samples:** GW-1 excavated Jiaotanxia Guan; GT-1 excavated Ming tomb at Jingdezhen, NOT a securely identified Song heirloom Ge. SEM-EDS and LA-ICP-MS. Brown residue dominated by Si/Al; inferred burial soil. Black GT-1 residue: EDS mean C76.48%, O20.98%, Si0.46% (proof pp.6–8, Table1; Figs.4–6). Ink is the authors’ inference, not molecular identification. No N detected; authors propose Py-GC-MS/Raman/FTIR to resolve identity.
- **Caption rather than fresh experiment:** Fig.1c identifies Longquan “literature Ge” with dense white cracks, black body, celadon glaze, image borrowed from Palace Museum2017. It is an old museum object; not evidence that fresh Longquan crackle is universally white.
- **Important:** source contains reversed glaze/body expansion wording in places. Use Li2001 measured CTE or accepted tensile-glaze mechanism, not this wording.

## F2 — direct modern ethnographic recipe; useful fresh transparency comparison

**Zihan Li. “Beyond Firing: The Chaîne Opératoire of Chinese Heirloom Ge Ware.” Archaeological Review from Cambridge 35(1),112–125 (2020).** [Original institutional PDF inspected](https://api.repository.cam.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/5cdad7f3-474d-4dea-b1bf-e71a9f656ec2/content). [Issue contents confirming pp.112–125](https://api.repository.cam.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/a93e4dcf-e208-4b37-8503-a9f109cb9ac4/content). No DOI located. Title varies slightly in bibliographies. Nanjing author profile gives91–109, contradicting actual issue contents; prefer112–125.

- **Own observation/interview:** Ye Kewei studio, Yunhe, Zhejiang, interview21 March2018. Fig.5 (PDF p.8, printed p.119) compares black stained exterior cracks against transparent interior cracks left without pigment. PDF p.9 / printed p.120 describes dipping fired work in carbon ink, waiting **3 days** for new fractures, then brown-soil burial usually **1 week**. This is a modern imitation procedure, not a surviving Song recipe.
- **Quoted experimental data:** Fig.3 (PDF p.5 / printed p.116) reprints Lahlil2015; text gives1mm glaze stopping about10days and5mm glaze continuing to31days. These figures are cited, not measured by Li. Fig.4 repeats crack-development images over6days.
- **Historical interpretation:** argument that heirloom Ge imitated buried Guan is a hypothesis, not demonstrated provenance/chronology. Avoid presenting “all heirloom Ge is Yuan imitation” as settled.
- **Source discrepancy:** Li2022’s review instead describes5days then1month, citing Li2020. Do not merge the two time sequences or call either an archaeological ageing rate.
- **Do not rely on burial anecdote:** Fig.7 / PDF p.11 identifies Palace Museum227527 as a tomb find with brown base and clear protected body; current museum page calls it Qing-palace old collection, without excavation story. The discrepancy remains unresolved.

## F3 — excavated Laohudong: intentional black versus burial gold (abstract only)

**吴昊泽 Wu Haoze, 李迎 Li Ying, 李志敏 Li Zhimin, 马清林 Ma Qinglin. “杭州老虎洞窑青瓷‘金丝铁线’成因机制研究” / “Formation mechanism of ‘golden wire and iron wire’ in celadons from Laohudong kiln in Hangzhou.” 北京化工大学学报（自然科学版）/ Journal of Beijing University of Chemical Technology52(5),133–144 (2025). DOI [10.13543/j.bhxbzr.2025.05.014](https://doi.org/10.13543/j.bhxbzr.2025.05.014). [Publisher](https://journal.buct.edu.cn/CN/10.13543/j.bhxbzr.2025.05.014).**

Publisher abstract reports original 3D microscopy, SEM-EDS, Raman on Laohudong sherds, compared with Nanhai I shipwreck celadon. Deposits mostly inside fissures; gold discontinuous, black continuous; some overlap. Laohudong gold phases: lepidocrocite(γ-FeOOH), hematite, quartz; black phases: biochar, pyrite, quartz. Authors infer burial deposits for gold and intentional decoration for black. Marine gold: hematite/quartz; marine black: pyrite/quartz, no detected biochar; both inferred burial products. Thus similar black/gold visual contrast can have different histories. Abstract does not establish every heirloom Ge or every Southern Song Guan was deliberately blackened. PDF shown13MB but link exposed only decorative PNG to web; full PDF unavailable so sample counts, stratigraphy, phase figure numbers, preparation details and widths remain unverified.

## F4 — Guan replicas and delayed cracks, post-fire two-stage staining

**Li Jiazhi, Deng Zequn, Xu Jiming. “Technical Studies and Replication of Guan Ware, an Ancient Chinese Ceramic.” MRS Bulletin26(1),31–37 (2001). DOI [10.1557/mrs2001.16](https://doi.org/10.1557/mrs2001.16). [CAS-hosted PDF inspected](https://www.sic.cas.cn/zt/lijiazhi/lwzz/201905/P020190513376253648067.pdf).**

- **Authors’ replication-based statement:** pp.35–36: cracks initiate during cooling and continue later. They *believe* black staining shortly after kiln removal, followed later by ginger staining of newly formed fractures, explains two-color crackle. Not a residue analysis or documented Song workshop recipe; no stain substance or elapsed time specified.
- **Own measured mechanical support:** p.34, CTE20–500°C body5.70×10^-6K^-1, glaze6.19×10^-6K^-1: glaze greater, tensile. This is consistent with craze formation.
- **Original fired appearance, not necessarily ageing:** p.34, Fig.6(bubbles/anorthite74×) and Fig.7(1500×BSE/EDS): replicas reproduce scattering/jade quality via microcrystals and bubbles. This is useful for the parent’s surface section, but do not double-count the200-word source summary allowance.

## F5 — tea and iron solutions, explicitly later practice

**Jessica Harrison-Hall. Catalogue of Late Yuan and Ming Ceramics in the British Museum (2001), catalogue9:3; quoted on British Museum object1947,0712.87 “ewer”. [Museum record](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/A_1947-0712-87), Curator’s comments.**

Ming/Jiajing probable ewer1522–1566, thick matte blue-white glaze and broad brown crackle. Curatorial statement says an imitation crackle could be produced by staining with iron solution or tea, popular in Qing. This is a museum scholarly historical statement, not analytical identification of tea/iron in the object and not evidence for tea being original Song Guan/Ge practice. No time-to-colour or concentration given.

**National Palace Museum, Taipei. “官窯 青瓷蓮瓣碗 / Bowl with lotus-petal decoration in celadon glaze, Guan ware”, object故瓷008711N000000000, datedSouthern Song–Yuan13th–14thC. [Digital record](https://digitalarchive.npm.gov.tw/Collection/Detail/27596?dep=U), description.** No named record author/date. References: 余佩瑾 Yu Pei-chin, 貴似晨星:清宮傳世12至14世紀青瓷特展,2016; 蔡和璧 Tsai Ho-pi,南宋官窯特展,1989.

Own collection description: grey-brown crackle interspersed with a few transparent ice cracks. Body/glaze compared with Laohudong excavated specimens. Useful observational counterexample to a model where all old cracks automatically darken; no dated formation sequence or stain analysis is supplied.

## F7 — delayed crazing general conservation authority

**University of Illinois, Preservation Self-Assessment Program, “Ceramic”, sectionChemical Deterioration. [Institutional guide](https://psap.library.illinois.edu/advanced-help/advhelp-ceramics).** No named author/year.

Guidance (not a Guan experiment): craze lines may be visible immediately after firing or emerge long afterwards; disrupted glaze permits the body to stain. Supports possibility of late crazing, not a Guan-specific growth curve or colour evolution over years.

## F8 — directly experimental crack-pattern study, full-text access incomplete

**Sophia Lahlil, Jiming Xu, Weidong Li. “Influence of manufacturing parameters on the crackling process of ancient Chinese glazed ceramics.” Journal of Cultural Heritage16(4),401–412 (2015). DOI [10.1016/j.culher.2014.10.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.culher.2014.10.003). [Publisher abstract and preview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1296207414001460).**

Original experimental model systems and23 archaeological Song/Yuan sherds(Jiaotanxia/Laohudong/Fenghuang). Key controlled influences: glaze/body relative thickness, firing temperature, atmosphere. Published preview accessible but full results not read; time values cited in Li2020 should remain “quoted from Lahlil2015”, pending source figure verification. Introduction says major Ge cracks deliberately black, minor gold-brown, but cites older art-history source[2]; not itself a staining chemistry measurement.

## Remaining gaps / limits

- No controlled fresh Guan/Ge/Longquan spectrophotometry or BRDF proving white, silvery, glittering or nearly invisible appearance located. Transparent/colorless is strongest explicit fresh description, but that frequently repeated claim has secondary citation chains. Li2020 Fig.5 offers direct modern clear/black visual comparison.
- No trustworthy longitudinal record showing centuries of heirloom Guan/Ge use changing individual lines, nor quantified contributions of skin oil, tea, dust and handling.
- No evidence that an heirloom necessarily has tea-stained golden lines; no chemical tea identification in a Song Guan/Ge crack located.
- No verifiable original Song recipe for black ink/iron/tea dyeing found. Modern recipes demonstrate possibility; archaeological pigment studies support intentional carbon in specific studied objects only.
- Soft pale-yellow130µm Met48503 band: source results do not establish its chemistry, original opening, alteration-layer thickness, or age chronology.
