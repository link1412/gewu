# Item 2 research notes: stain, crack walls, alteration depths

Working conclusion (2026-10-29): stain is not restricted to an intact slit. Primary glaze cross-sections show corrosion beside cracks and deposition inside corrosion-created pore networks; however, there is no established basis yet to identify the ~130 µm optical band with a 130 µm glass alteration zone. Distinguish (a) crack aperture, (b) deposits on/in the slit and connected pores, (c) chemically altered glaze thickness measured normal to a wetted surface, and (d) apparent width after subsurface light transport.

## Sources read / evidence captured

### W1 — direct Longquan crack-adjacent corrosion; no simple uniform leached layer

**Yan He, Weidong Li, Jianan Li, Changsong Xu, Xiaoke Lu.** “Corrosion of Longquan celadons in the marine environment: study on the celadons from the Dalian Island shipwreck of the Yuan Dynasty.” *Heritage Science* **9**, 104 (2021). DOI [10.1186/s40494-021-00583-4](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40494-021-00583-4); [publisher](https://www.nature.com/articles/s40494-021-00583-4).

- **Measured/observed in this study:** two severely degraded Yuan Longquan shards, among 11 examined. Fig. **7b–i**: corrosion region beside a through-glaze crack; porous glass at crack-adjacent interface; yellow area follows the crack inward; yellow material also occupies pores. Fig. 9 and Tables 6–7: Mg/Al-rich secondary material, XRD consistent with quintinite; some internal deposits Fe-rich. These are not a direct demonstration of FeOOH diffusion into dense pristine glass.
- **Author interpretation, not measured layer thickness:** Discussion “Corrosion process of celadon” explicitly says **no dealkalinised layer formed** at the surface in these samples; glass-network dissolution dominates. Ca-rich separated glass/crystal interfaces corrode preferentially, create pores and expose anorthite, then crystals detach. Fig. 11 is a proposed sequence, not a time series.
- **Scale:** authors describe final holes as tens of µm; this is pit size, not a 130 µm crack halo. No calibrated lateral halo width or time-growth law is supplied.
- **Transfer limit:** exceptional badly corroded marine opaque/matt Longquan; not a direct model for mild Guan soil burial or heirloom wear.

### W2 — Fe oxyhydroxides can accumulate in porous altered glaze and connected bubbles

**Yanzi Wang, Yongbin Yu, Naisheng Li, Qiang Wu, Xiaolin Cheng.** “Corrosion mechanisms of a bluish-white porcelain of Hutian kiln excavated from Nanhai No. 1 shipwreck.” *npj Heritage Science* **13**, 359 (2025), 13 pp. DOI [10.1038/s40494-025-01945-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s40494-025-01945-y); [publisher](https://www.nature.com/articles/s40494-025-01945-y).

- **Measured here (pp. 6–8):** Fig. **9**, Table 5 distinguish silica-rich particulate inner corrosion front from Fe-rich intermediate/outer deposits. Fig. **10d / 11b**: Fe-rich contaminants fill a semicircular gap below residual glaze. Connected bubbles and microcracks provide routes and deposition sites (Figs. 8c, 10). Raman, Fig. **12**: brown reflective region **β-FeOOH**; brown matte region **α-FeOOH + β-FeOOH**.
- **Authors' inference:** porous amorphous-silica aggregate accommodates contaminants; microcracks can arise during ion exchange or post-recovery dehydration. This supports contaminant entry into altered pore space, not molecular diffusion of pigment across unaltered dense glass.
- **No usable layer thickness / growth law captured.** Marine bluish-white porcelain and Fe-rich shipwreck concretion, not terrestrial Guan. Do not identify the pale-yellow band as β-FeOOH from colour alone.

### W3 — quantified terrestrial glaze corrosion depth (Ru analogue)

**Benyuan Zhou, Qinglin Ma, Zhimin Li, Jiachang Chen.** “Corrosion Research on Ru Porcelain Glazes Excavated at Qingliangsi, Baofeng, China.” *Coatings* **13**(6), 1011 (2023). DOI [10.3390/coatings13061011](https://doi.org/10.3390/coatings13061011); [publisher](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-6412/13/6/1011).

- **Measured in this study:** two excavated Ru glazes. Table 1 SiO₂ **59.5–60.3 wt%**, Al₂O₃ **16.3–16.4%**, CaO **14.7–15.2%**, K₂O **3.2–3.6%**: closer to calcium–alkali celadon than many medieval window-glass analogues. §3.2 / Figs. **13c–g, 14d–f** report corrosion around the anorthite/glass boundary approximately **40 µm inward from the surface** (3-D connected Ca-rich phase); second-type corrosion along linear droplet rows reaches approximately **100 µm inward from the surface** (Fig. S1b, 13b,h,14b; verified primary body text). The conclusion loosely calls it approximately twice as deep; use actual ~40 and ~100 µm values, not an inferred 80 µm.
- **Observed / author mechanism:** preferential dissolution of Ca-rich domains leaves Si-rich interconnected residue; yellow soil contaminants enter degraded glaze; white pits and exposed crystals change scattering. This is heterogeneous corrosion depth normal to surface, **not a crack-face halo width**. No rate-versus-time experiment; age nearly 1000 years does not establish linear growth.

### W4 — Fe can migrate within a porous glass-alteration gel (not direct Guan evidence)

**A. Michelin, E. Burger, D. Rebiscoul, D. Neff, F. Bruguier, E. Drouet, P. Dillmann, S. Gin.** “Silicate Glass Alteration Enhanced by Iron: Origin and Long-Term Implications.” *Environmental Science & Technology* **47**(2), 750–756 (2013). DOI [10.1021/es304057y](https://doi.org/10.1021/es304057y); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23237387/).

- **Primary abstract read, full text not yet obtained:** multiscale comparison of short-term nuclear-glass/iron interface with long-term archaeological glass/iron interface; STXM and other methods show Fe migration within porous gel and Fe-silicate precipitation. Supports a chemically reactive porous alteration zone, not stain penetration into intact dense glaze. The precipitates are **Fe silicates**, not an identification of FeOOH in Guan.
- **Not accepted as quantitative evidence yet:** secondary review says archaeological age 450 years and laboratory 756 days, but numerical thicknesses must be checked in primary full text before reporting as primary measurements.

### W5 — 52-year field experiment separates leached depth from Fe-rich alteration deposit

**C. L. Thorpe, A. J. Fisher, G. Manifold, S. Creasey-Gray, C. M. Jackson, B. Stone, C. L. Corkhill, C. Boothman, J. R. Lloyd, R. J. Hand.** “Insights into long term glass corrosion mechanisms from the Ballidon experiment.” *npj Materials Degradation* **9**, 27 (2025), 22 pp. DOI [10.1038/s41529-025-00571-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41529-025-00571-0); [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11913731/). [Original publisher PDF](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/224797/1/s41529-025-00571-0.pdf) is too large for web tool (11.7 MB); text extracts archived at `papers/weather_ballidon2025.web.txt`.

- **Measured here:** 52-year burial at Ballidon, limestone rubble/soil, initially alkaline then measured pH **7.8–8.2** in 2022. Fig. **9** / Table **7**: Roman simulant shows intermittent Fe-rich alteration **1–3 µm** thick, no continuous gel; EPMA shows sodium depletion in first **5 µm**. Authors infer amorphous Fe(III) oxyhydroxide within silica gel from Fe/Si **1.5–3.3**; **this mineral identification is an interpretation from chemistry, not direct Raman/diffraction proof**. Table **10**: soda–lime optical glass alteration **5–10 µm**; medieval low-silica potash glass **40–100 µm**; 17th-century low-silica type **300–1000 µm**. Normal to exposed surface, not a measured Guan crack halo.
- **Composition from an earlier source, explicitly quoted here:** Table **6** compositions are reported from **Fletcher 1972**, not newly measured bulk composition: Roman simulant **68.5 wt% SiO₂, 18.2 Na₂O, 7.3 CaO, 3.6 Al₂O₃**; soda–lime optical **70.1 SiO₂, 10.6 Na₂O, 5.1 K₂O, 9.5 CaO, 0.8 Al₂O₃**. Celadon has much higher Al₂O₃ and lower Na₂O, so these are mechanism/scale analogues, not matching-composition rate standards.
- **Quoted from earlier study, NOT newly measured time series:** Thorpe et al. Table **3** / Discussion cite **S. D. McLoughlin, R. J. Hand, N. C. Hyatt, W. E. Lee, I. Notingher, D. S. McPhail, J. Henderson**, “The long term corrosion of glasses: analytical results after 32 years of burial at Ballidon”, *Glass Technology: European Journal of Glass Science and Technology Part A* **47**, 59–67 (2006): Na-depleted Roman-glass zone **~2 µm (1 y), ~6 µm (9 y), 10–20 µm (32 y)**, silicon structural changes outer **2–5 µm** at 32 y. Thorpe notes depth/position of old samples unspecified; **do not fit a monotone law through the 52-year ~5 µm and earlier values**.
- **Author conclusion:** alteration-layer volume need not equal dissolved-glass volume: drying separates lamellae, density changes, and external porewater species add new secondary phases. Surface finish and moisture also matter. The 52-y low-silica samples show layer growth and water re-entry through cracks rather than guaranteed permanent passivation.

### W6 — a primary laboratory time series (illustrates conditional growth, not an age conversion)

**Thalie Law, Odile Majérus, Marie Godet, Mélanie Moskura, Thibault Charpentier, Antoine Seyeux, Daniel Caurant.** “Does Water Cleaning Mitigate Atmospheric Degradation of Unstable Heritage Glass? An Experimental Study on Glass Models.” *Heritage* **8**(7), 276 (2025), 29 pp. DOI [10.3390/heritage8070276](https://doi.org/10.3390/heritage8070276); [publisher](https://www.mdpi.com/2571-9408/8/7/276).

- **Measured here:** low-lime unstable soda-lime silicate (~74 wt% SiO₂, ~19 Na₂O, ~6 CaO+MgO) aged at **35 °C, 85% RH**. §**3.2.2**, Fig. **13** cross-sectional SEM-BSE: hydrated layer **2.5 ± 0.5 µm at six months**, **8.5 ± 0.5 µm after 21 months** in unrinsed sample; water-rinsed counterpart remained **2.5 ± 0.5 µm** after same continuation. §3.1.2, Fig.8: ToF-SIMS tracks exogenous K in layer, probable supply from RH-buffer KCl; after rinsing apparent layer shrinks ~800 to600nm. Thus ions can penetrate a transformed layer.
- **Authors' kinetic interpretation:** under these particular unrinsed accelerated conditions hydration approximately **linear, ~0.4 µm/month** because network hydrolysis controls continuing water entry. It is **not** a universal square-root or linear age law; do not extrapolate 21-month test over 800 years or equate it to Guan glaze (much more Al, much less Na, more Ca).
- Treatment outcomes here concern model glass; not a conservation recommendation for Guan.

## Mechanistic modelling guidance (inference, not measurement)

Use a narrow actual fracture plus independently controlled deposit coverage/colour, porous wall-adjacent corrosion and the bulk glaze's subsurface transport. The data justify the *possibility* of an optically soft, wider crack-associated zone. They do **not** justify setting each wall's alteration depth to 65 µm merely because the photographed line band is130 µm wide; neither a symmetric section nor the optical point-spread/subsurface contribution has been measured. A bright band may involve additional scattering (porosity, microcracks, exposed crystals), while iron contamination can absorb; band brightness alone cannot identify the balance or mineral.

### Optional W7 — buried crack filling can broaden the visible feature without proving lateral dye diffusion

Web extract archived `papers/weather_zanini2025.web.txt`.

- **Observed here:** Roman natron silica-soda-lime glass from plough soil near Aquileia. §3.1 pp.4–7, Figs.2–5 microCT distinguishes voids, soil material and glass.
