# Brief: what age does to Guan/Ge crackle and glaze (fresh vs heirloom vs excavated)

For a film project we render Southern Song Guan glaze (官窑, thick bubbly celadon over a dark body). Our reference photo is an excavated sherd about 800 years old (Met 48503); its crack lines are soft pale-yellow bands about 130 um wide, a little brighter than the glaze. We want one model that can give a piece fresh from the kiln, an heirloom piece (传世) and an excavated one (出土), so we need to know which parts of the look come from age, with sources.

1. Fresh crackle: how do crackle lines look on a newly fired Guan/Ge-type or Longquan celadon glaze before any staining (colourless, white, silvery, glittering, nearly invisible)? Is the colour of 金丝铁线 natural (soil, use, handling) or deliberate (soaking in tea, ink, iron solutions), for heirloom Ge/Guan versus excavated Guan (Laohudong, Jiaotanxia)? Any description of how the colour develops over years (delayed crazing that stays pale, lines that darken with use).
2. How a stain gets into and around a crack: does it only coat the crack faces and fill the mouth, or does it enter the glass beside the crack (a hydrated / leached / weathered layer on the crack faces, iron oxyhydroxide precipitated in it)? Thickness of such layers on old glazes or archaeological glass of similar composition (high-silica, calcium-alkali glass), and how they grow with time.
3. Surface weathering: gloss, roughness or iridescence of excavated versus heirloom versus modern Guan/Ge/Longquan glaze (gloss units, roughness Ra, SEM of the surface). Is the satin ("酥油光") look of Guan glaze original (bubbles, crystals, firing) or partly age?

Deliverable: `report.md` in this directory (write it as you go): per item, the source (authors, title, venue, year, DOI or URL, page or figure), the numbers or statements, and whether each was measured in that source or quoted from elsewhere. Downloaded papers and images in their own folders. Say plainly what you could not find.
