# What age does to Guan/Ge crackle and glaze

Evidence review for a film material model, 29 October 2026. Focus: thick Southern Song Guan glaze over a dark body; related Ge and Longquan material is identified separately.

## Findings that should guide the model

Use **one material system with separate firing, crack formation, staining, and weathering controls**, rather than a single age-to-colour/roughness slider. The evidence below supports these conclusions:

1. Fresh cracks need not contain a dark or yellow pigment. Deliberate post-firing colouring is documented in modern Ge reconstruction; archaeological crack deposits can instead come from burial. Neither explanation covers every heirloom or excavated object (§1).
2. Contamination can occupy the crack **and corrosion-created pores beside it**. A model restricted to a coloured slit is therefore incomplete for some buried glazes. Conversely, pigment diffusion through pristine dense glass is not established (§2).
3. Jade-like body scattering can originate in firing. Burial can add pits, loss of gloss, porous residues and delamination, but some archaeological Longquan glaze remains shiny. There is no controlled Guan fresh/heirloom/excavated gloss curve (§3).
4. The brief's **~130 µm pale-yellow bright band is an apparent photographic width**, not a measured aperture or alteration-layer thickness. A wider scattering zone is plausible; its dimensions and chemistry on this sherd remain unverified (§4).

## 1. Fresh crackle, delayed crazing, and 金丝铁线

### 1.1 Fresh Guan: explicit description exists, but check its evidence level [F1]

**Zihan Li, Feng Yuan, Jianwen Cao, Anke Hein. “Insights into the Residue Trapped in Glaze Cracks of Archaeological Ceramics Using Microchemical Analysis.” Microscopy and Microanalysis 28(6), 1878–1889 (2022).** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1431927622012375), [Oxford manuscript](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A4147a86e-094d-44e3-9d39-03c89b300e47/files/s2b88qd441). Locators: **Discussion / “Coloring Mechanisms,” author-proof p. 8 (approximately final p. 1885); Table 1, Figs. 4–6**. Read through author-uploaded proof/indexed manuscript; final pagination not independently checked.

**Q:** newly made Guan cracks are described as **transparent/colorless**; many never-buried Palace Museum Guan are said to retain transparent cracks. That paragraph cites Chen et al. (1984), Wu et al. (2009), Miao et al. (2012); it is **not this study's fresh firing experiment**. Those original fresh-appearance passages were not independently verified.

**M:** Jiaotanxia Guan **GW-1** contains Si/Al-rich brown residue; Ming tomb Ge-type **GT-1** has carbon-rich black residue (EDS means **C 76.48%, O 20.98%, Si 0.46%**). **A:** brown material is soil-derived; black may be ink. Ink was not molecularly identified. GT-1 is neither securely identified Song heirloom Ge nor representative of every Ge object. Dense white cracks shown on an old Longquan example in Fig. 1c are not a fresh-state observation.

### 1.2 Direct modern comparison: transparent cracks become black with ink [F2]

**Zihan Li. “Beyond Firing: The Chaîne Opératoire of Chinese Heirloom Ge Ware.” Archaeological Review from Cambridge 35(1), 112–125 (2020).** [Institutional PDF](https://api.repository.cam.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/5cdad7f3-474d-4dea-b1bf-e71a9f656ec2/content), [issue contents](https://api.repository.cam.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/a93e4dcf-e208-4b37-8503-a9f109cb9ac4/content). No DOI located. Locators: **p. 119 Fig. 5; p. 120** (PDF pages 8–9).

**M, modern workshop observation/interview:** Ye Kewei's studio in Yunhe, Zhejiang; Fig. 5 contrasts **transparent unstained interior cracks** with **black ink-stained exterior cracks**. The account describes carbon-ink immersion, **three days** for later cracks, then brown-soil exposure usually **one week**. This directly demonstrates a fresh-to-coloured mechanism in a modern imitation, not an original Song recipe. Li et al. (2022) retells the sequence as five days/one month; do not merge these accounts into a measured historical rate. Li's broader proposal that heirloom Ge imitated buried Guan is an archaeological hypothesis, not settled chronology. A separate burial anecdote in this paper was excluded because its provenance conflicts with the current museum record (see working notes).

### 1.3 Excavated Laohudong: similar colours can have different causes [F3]

**吴昊泽 (Haoze Wu), 李迎 (Ying Li), 李志敏 (Zhimin Li), 马清林 (Qinglin Ma). “杭州老虎洞窑青瓷‘金丝铁线’成因机制研究” / “Formation mechanism of ‘golden wire and iron wire’ in celadons from Laohudong kiln in Hangzhou.” 北京化工大学学报（自然科学版）/ Journal of Beijing University of Chemical Technology 52(5), 133–144 (2025).** [DOI/publisher](https://doi.org/10.13543/j.bhxbzr.2025.05.014). Locator: **publisher abstract only**; full PDF/sample stratigraphy/figure numbers not verified.

**M, as reported in the abstract:** 3D microscopy, SEM/EDS and Raman find deposits mainly inside cracks. Laohudong gold is discontinuous; black is more continuous. Gold contains **lepidocrocite (γ-FeOOH), hematite, quartz**; black contains **biochar, pyrite, quartz**. **A:** gold is a burial-corrosion deposit, black an intentionally controlled decoration. Comparison Nanhai No. 1 celadon instead has gold hematite/quartz and black pyrite/quartz without detected biochar; authors infer burial origins for both there. Thus black colour is not itself evidence of ink, and excavation does not exclude original deliberate colouring. This abstract cannot establish that all its Laohudong pieces are Southern Song rather than Yuan, identify every heirloom Ge's process, or quantify an altered crack-wall layer.

### 1.4 Tea and iron solutions: documented later practice, not proven Song practice [F4]

**Jessica Harrison-Hall. Catalogue of Late Yuan and Ming Ceramics in the British Museum (British Museum Press, 2001), catalogue 9:3**, quoted in [British Museum ewer 1947,0712.87](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/A_1947-0712-87), “Curator's comments.”

**A/Q, historical curatorial statement:** staining with **iron solution or tea** could produce imitation crackle coloration and was popular in the **Qing dynasty**. The catalogued ewer is probable Jiajing, **1522–1566**, Jingdezhen, with matte blue-white glaze/brown crackle. This is not a chemical analysis identifying tea in that ewer or an original Song Guan/Ge workshop record. No concentration, penetration thickness, or years-to-colour is supplied.

### 1.5 Delayed crazing does not prescribe delayed staining [F5]

**Sophia Lahlil, Jiming Xu, Weidong Li. “Influence of manufacturing parameters on the crackling process of ancient Chinese glazed ceramics.” Journal of Cultural Heritage 16(4), 401–412 (2015).** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.culher.2014.10.003), [publisher preview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1296207414001460). **Q, numerical results checked only through Li (2020), p. 116 / Fig. 3:** approximately **1 mm** model glaze stops forming cracks after **ten days**, whereas **5 mm** glaze continues to **31 days**. These are model/experimental values quoted by Li, not measurements newly made by Li or a centuries-long Guan record. The original full results were not independently accessible. Thickness, firing and atmosphere matter; do not transfer these days directly to an archaeological object.

Li/Deng/Xu's replication paper **[S2], pp. 35–36**, also describes cracks continuing after cooling and proposes sequential black then ginger staining. That is the authors' reconstruction, not measured historical colour evolution. The [University of Illinois PSAP “Ceramic” guide](https://psap.library.illinois.edu/advanced-help/advhelp-ceramics), **“Chemical Deterioration,” undated**, states that crazing can appear immediately or long after firing; this is conservation guidance, not Guan-specific kinetics.

### 1.6 An old object can retain clear cracks [F6]

**National Palace Museum, Taipei, “官窯 青瓷蓮瓣碗 / Bowl with lotus-petal decoration in celadon glaze, Guan ware,”** object **故瓷008711N000000000**, dated Southern Song–Yuan, **13th–14th century**. [Museum record](https://digitalarchive.npm.gov.tw/Collection/Detail/27596?dep=U), “Description,” undated; accessed 29 October 2026.

**A, collection observation:** grey-brown crackle is interspersed with a few **transparent ice cracks**. This is a useful counterexample to uniform age-darkening. The record gives neither formation dates for individual cracks nor residue chemistry, so it does not prove the transparent lines formed centuries later or remained pale for a known duration.

### 1.7 White is not a unique fresh-state signature; Ge labels need care [F7]

**Sabrina Rastelli. “The Polyhedral and Elusive Nature of Geyao.” Annali di Ca' Foscari. Serie orientale 57, 605–626 (2021).** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.30687/AnnOr/2385-3042/2021/01/022), [publisher PDF](https://edizionicafoscari.unive.it/media/pdf/article/annali-di-ca-foscari-serie-orientale/2021/1/art-10.30687-AnnOr-2385-3042-2021-01-022.pdf). Locators: **pp. 610–611; p. 619, footnote 25; Figs. 12–13 on p. 618**.

**A/Q:** pronounced whitish crackles on excavated black-bodied Longquan are proposed as burial discoloration; footnote 25 credits a theory shared by Nigel Wood. This is **not** measured hydration, identified white mineral, or a measured layer thickness. Her documentary analysis distinguishes “museum/heirloom Ge” from excavated Laohudong and Longquan groups, and questions treating double crackle and its familiar name as a timeless fixed definition. Use the material/site/date, not simply “Ge,” when transferring evidence.

**Answer to the appearance question:** transparent/nonpigmented is the best-supported starting point; the direct modern evidence is clearer than the ancient citation chain. Universal fresh **white, silver, glittering or nearly invisible** appearance is not measured by the sources found. Such words may describe lighting-dependent fracture optics or weathered/deposited material, and must not be treated as equivalent.

## 2. What can enter a crack and the glass beside it

### 2.1 Crack, deposit and altered wall are different structures

**I, synthesis:** distinguish four lengths: the physical open aperture; the width/coverage of deposited material; alteration depth measured normal to a wetted crack face or exposed glaze surface; and the apparent band width after viewing geometry, subsurface scattering, focus and image processing. They need not coincide. Environmental ions/particles can be transported into an already porous altered region; that is different from a dissolved dye moving laterally through intact glass.

### 2.2 Direct Longquan evidence: corrosion beside cracks [W1]

**Yan He, Weidong Li, Jianan Li, Changsong Xu, Xiaoke Lu. “Corrosion of Longquan celadons in the marine environment: study on the celadons from the Dalian Island shipwreck of the Yuan Dynasty.” Heritage Science 9, 104 (2021).** [DOI/publisher](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40494-021-00583-4). Locators: **Fig. 7b–i; Fig. 9; Tables 6–7; discussion “Corrosion process of celadon”; Fig. 11**.

**M:** severely degraded sherds have a yellow corrosion region beside a through-glaze crack, porous glass at the interface, and deposits in pores. SEM/EDS/XRD identify heterogeneous secondary material, including Mg/Al-rich material consistent with quintinite; some deposits are Fe-rich. **A:** preferential dissolution of Ca-rich domains and crystal/glass interfaces creates connected cavities; crystals can subsequently detach. The authors explicitly report **no dealkalinised surface layer** in these specimens: network dissolution, rather than a universal uniform leached rind, is their explanation. Holes of **tens of micrometres** are pit dimensions, not a measured lateral crack halo. Fig. 11 is a proposed sequence, not a time series. This is a severe marine Longquan analogue, not direct evidence about mild soil-buried Guan or Met 48503.

### 2.3 Fe oxyhydroxides in altered porous glaze [W2]

**Yanzi Wang, Yongbin Yu, Naisheng Li, Qiang Wu, Xiaolin Cheng. “Corrosion mechanisms of a bluish-white porcelain of Hutian kiln excavated from Nanhai No. 1 shipwreck.” npj Heritage Science 13, 359 (2025).** [DOI/publisher](https://doi.org/10.1038/s40494-025-01945-y). Locators: **Figs. 8–12, Table 5**.

**M:** SEM/EDS distinguish a silica-rich inner corrosion front and Fe-rich deposits; connected bubbles, microcracks and a gap beneath remaining glaze provide contaminant pathways/sites. Raman (**Fig. 12**) identifies **β-FeOOH** in a brown reflective region and **α-FeOOH plus β-FeOOH** in a brown matte region. **A:** contaminants accumulate in the porous silica aggregate left by degradation. This demonstrates that iron staining need not stay in the original crack mouth. It does **not** identify the pale Guan band as these minerals, show diffusion through dense glass, or measure a Guan crack-wall layer. The specimen is marine Hutian bluish-white porcelain, with a different composition/exposure history. No transferable thickness or growth law was obtained from it.

### 2.4 A terrestrial celadon comparison: Ru corrosion depths [W3]

**Benyuan Zhou, Qinglin Ma, Zhimin Li, Jiachang Chen. “Corrosion Research on Ru Porcelain Glazes Excavated at Qingliangsi, Baofeng, China.” Coatings 13(6), 1011 (2023).** [DOI/full text](https://doi.org/10.3390/coatings13061011). Locators: **Table 1; §3.2; Figs. 13c–g, 14d–f**.

**M:** two excavated Ru glazes contain **59.5–60.3 wt% SiO₂, 16.3–16.4% Al₂O₃, 14.7–15.2% CaO, 3.2–3.6% K₂O**. Corrosion along anorthite/glass boundaries extends about **40 µm inward from the exposed surface**; another droplet-row morphology extends about **100 µm** (**Fig. S1b; Figs. 13b/h, 14b**). The conclusion loosely calls this about twice as deep; 100 µm is the stated value, not an inferred 80 µm. **A:** preferential loss of Ca-rich material leaves a Si-rich framework and permits yellow soil contamination; pits/exposed crystals also change the optical appearance. These heterogeneous penetration depths are a useful order-of-magnitude celadon analogue, not a hydrated-film thickness, crack aperture, or bilateral halo measurement. No known exposure history or measured rate establishes a depth after 800 years.

### 2.5 Quantified high-silica glass analogue: 52 years at Ballidon [W4]

**C. L. Thorpe, A. J. Fisher, G. Manifold, S. Creasey-Gray, C. M. Jackson, B. Stone, C. L. Corkhill, C. Boothman, J. R. Lloyd, R. J. Hand. “Insights into long term glass corrosion mechanisms from the Ballidon experiment.” npj Materials Degradation 9, 27 (2025).** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41529-025-00571-0), [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11913731/). Locators: **Tables 3, 6–7, 10; Fig. 9**.

**M, 52-year field burial:** Roman simulant has intermittent **1–3 µm** Fe-rich alteration and Na depletion through approximately **5 µm**. Soda–lime optical glass has **5–10 µm** alteration; low-silica medieval potash glass **40–100 µm**. **A:** Fe/Si chemistry supports amorphous Fe(III) oxyhydroxide within silica gel; this is an interpretation, not Raman mineral identification. Alteration-layer volume need not equal lost-glass volume.

**Q, not newly measured bulk compositions:** Table 6 uses Fletcher (1972): Roman simulant **68.5% SiO₂, 18.2% Na₂O, 7.3% CaO, 3.6% Al₂O₃**; optical glass **70.1% SiO₂, 10.6% Na₂O, 5.1% K₂O, 9.5% CaO, 0.8% Al₂O₃** (wt%). These have substantially less Al and more Na than Guan. Earlier Na-depletion values are quoted from McLoughlin et al. (2006), not a matched continuous growth curve; they must not be combined with the 52-year endpoint to fit Guan kinetics.

### 2.6 How layers grow: a controlled time series, with strict transfer limits [W5]

**Thalie Law, Odile Majérus, Marie Godet, Mélanie Moskura, Thibault Charpentier, Antoine Seyeux, Daniel Caurant. “Does Water Cleaning Mitigate Atmospheric Degradation of Unstable Heritage Glass? An Experimental Study on Glass Models.” Heritage 8(7), 276 (2025).** [DOI/full text](https://doi.org/10.3390/heritage8070276). Locators: **§3.2.2, Fig. 13; §3.1.2, Fig. 8**.

**M:** unstable low-lime soda–lime glass aged at **35 °C, 85% RH** developed a hydrated layer of **2.5 ± 0.5 µm after six months**, growing to **8.5 ± 0.5 µm after 21 months** without rinsing. A rinsed counterpart stayed at about **2.5 ± 0.5 µm**. ToF-SIMS also found exogenous K in the altered layer. **A:** approximately linear hydration, **~0.4 µm/month**, under this particular test arises from continuing network hydrolysis. This is evidence of a mechanism and environmental dependence, not a Guan age calibration or conservation recommendation. It cannot be extrapolated across 800 years. No single linear or square-root law is justified for the requested three material states.

### 2.7 A porous gel can chemically react with incoming Fe [W6]

**A. Michelin, E. Burger, D. Rebiscoul, D. Neff, F. Bruguier, E. Drouet, P. Dillmann, S. Gin. “Silicate Glass Alteration Enhanced by Iron: Origin and Long-Term Implications.” Environmental Science & Technology 47(2), 750–756 (2013).** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/es304057y), [primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23237387/).

**M/A, abstract only verified:** comparative multiscale study of short-term glass/iron and archaeological glass/iron interfaces reports Fe migration within a porous alteration gel and Fe-silicate precipitation. The phases here are **Fe silicates**, not direct Guan FeOOH evidence. **Gap:** full-text layer dimensions were not verified, so numbers quoted by secondary reviews are not used here.

### 2.8 Optical width can also include subsurface filling [W7]

**Roberta Zanini, Giulia Franceschin, Luisa Vigorelli, Gianluca Iori, Lara Chiaberge, Elena Longo, Laura Guidorzi, Alessandro Re, Alessandro Lo Giudice, Arianna Traviglia. “Laboratory and synchrotron x-ray micro–computed tomography to shed light on degradation features of corroded Roman glass.” Journal of the American Ceramic Society 108(3), e20241 (2025; online 2024).** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/jace.20241). Locators: **pp. 4–7, §3.1; Fig. 3D–F**.

**M:** microCT of Roman soda–lime glass links visible white regions to soil-filled subsurface cracks beneath surviving glass. **A:** a low-density outline may be hydrated silica, including along crack walls; CT alone does not chemically prove that identification. This provides an alternative to interpreting every broad pale feature as lateral dye diffusion. It is heavily damaged Roman glass, not a measured Guan halo, and supplies no transferable age-width law.

## 3. Surface weathering: gloss, roughness, iridescence and 酥油光

### 3.1 Actual gloss measurements exist, but not an ageing comparison [S1]

**于欢 (Huan Yu), 陈猛 (Meng Chen), 周健儿 (Jianer Zhou), 董晓烽 (Xiaofeng Dong), 程家太 (Jiatai Cheng). “南宋官窑与龙泉哥窑釉面成分对比分析” [Comparative analysis of glaze composition of Southern Song Guan and Longquan Ge]. 陶瓷学报 / Journal of Ceramics 42(6), 1072–1079 (2021).** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.13957/j.cnki.tcxb.2021.06.022), [official abstract](https://qkzzs.jcu.edu.cn/info/1011/4315.htm), [PDF](https://www.sciengine.com/doi/pdf/75869AEA265B403BACB28065DF5A359D). Locators: **p. 1073 methods; p. 1074 Table 1; Fig. 1**.

**M, archaeological sherds, mean “Gloss” values:**

| Group | Colour label | n | Reported gloss |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Jiaotanxia Southern Song Guan | 粉青 | 2 | 46.70 |
| Longquan Ge | 粉青 | 38 | 60.56 |
| Jiaotanxia Southern Song Guan | 米黄 | 1 | 45.20 |
| Longquan Ge | 米黄 | 6 | 64.03 |

The **47 sherds** comprise **3 Jiaotanxia, 24 Dayao, 20 Xikou Wayaoyang** pieces. Instrument: **Hanpu HP-300 single-angle glossmeter**; the angle/calibration were not verified in accessible methods. Do not silently assign 60° GU. **M/A:** Guan has dense **~10 µm** bubbles and fine quartz; Longquan bubbles are generally tens–hundreds of µm and sparser. Authors associate these firing structures/vitrification with the gloss difference. This is **not** fresh versus aged, heirloom versus excavated, or all museum Ge; **n = 1–2** for Guan is a major limit. No Ra was reported in the retrieved evidence.

### 3.2 Modern replication supports a firing-created jade quality [S2]

**Jiazhi Li, Zequn Deng, Jiming Xu. “Technical Studies and Replication of Guan Ware, an Ancient Chinese Ceramic.” MRS Bulletin 26(1), 31–37 (2001).** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1557/mrs2001.16), [institutional PDF](https://www.sic.cas.cn/zt/lijiazhi/lwzz/201905/P020190513376253648067.pdf). Locators: **pp. 33–36; Figs. 6–7**.

**M:** modern replicas contain residual quartz, small bubbles and anorthite; the anorthite microcrystals' analysed oxide composition (wt%) is **47.2% SiO₂, 15.7% CaO, 36.9% Al₂O₃**. **A, supported by replication:** these structures contribute the jade appearance. Raising firing temperature remelts anorthite and loses that appearance; the authors give **1240 °C** as an upper limit for their replication process. This provides a route to jade-like glaze without centuries of ageing, not proof of one historical firing schedule for every Guan object. The authors also say cracks start on cooling and continue growing later, without a calibrated years-long colour record. **Gap:** no natural-surface Ra or fresh-to-ancient GU comparison. Internal scattering must not be conflated with a rough external surface or a chemically weathered rind.

### 3.3 Recent Jiaotanxia study: useful microstructure, important preparation caveats [S3]

**Zhao Ren, Han Wang, Zongming Liu, Mengyi Zhang, Fen Wang, Jianfeng Zhu, Biao Zhang, Pei Shi. “The mechanisms of color, opacifying, and cracks in celadon glazes from the Southern Song Guan Kiln.” npj Heritage Science 14, 187 (2026).** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s40494-026-02383-0), [PDF](https://www.nature.com/articles/s40494-026-02383-0.pdf). Locators: **pp. 2–3 methods/Table 1; pp. 5–6 Figs. 6–8**.

**M:** six excavated Jiaotanxia specimens; Table 1 describes **G-2/G-3 cracks with no obvious colour**, while G-1/G-4/G-5/G-6 have dark-yellow cracks. That is ancient-state variation, not fresh-state observation. SEM phase-domain diameter estimates are **21, 28, 27, 22, 30, 22 nm**. **A:** crystals/bubbles and composition-dependent internal structure explain opacity and jade quality. **Caution:** text is inconsistent about the optical weight of very small phase domains; no precise scattering coefficients are supplied.

SEM used **1 vol% HF for 30 s**; XPS preparation removed **~50 µm** by sanding. Neither etched morphology nor removed thickness measures a natural weathered surface. Colour **L*** is not gloss. No GU, Ra, or age-controlled comparison is given.

### 3.4 Similar burial duration can leave both bright and degraded surfaces [S4]

**Yan He, Weidong Li, Jianan Li, Changsong Xu, Xiaoke Lu. “Research on the degradation of ancient Longquan celadons in the Dalian Island shipwreck.” npj Materials Degradation 6, 4 (2022).** [DOI/publisher](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41529-022-00217-5). Locators: **Methods / Supplementary Table 3; Figs. 1–3, 8–10**.

**M:** eleven Yuan shipwreck sherds include **01–05 bright as new, 06–07 lackluster, 08–10 matte, 11 underfired**. Some preserved transparent glazes show scratches/pits without obvious chemical corrosion; degraded examples show porous material, pitting and delamination. Samples were desalinated. **A:** original crystal-rich matte structure can be more corrosion-susceptible; physical damage and chemical attack interact. Thus present mattness may combine original firing texture and later deterioration. This is marine Longquan, not a Guan heirloom experiment. No GU or Ra values were obtained from this paper. Do not model all excavated glaze as matte or assume that a shiny old glaze must be modern.

### 3.5 What can be said about 酥油光 and iridescence

**I, synthesis of S1–S4:** “酥油光” is not a standard measured optical category in the retrieved studies. Its perceived soft, oily/jade-like character can combine a coherent surface reflection with depth scattering. Firing-created bubbles/crystals supply evidence for an original contribution; weathering can add diffuse reflection and reduce specular coherence. Their relative contributions on a particular heirloom object remain unmeasured. A shader should preserve those as independent controls.

**Gap:** no verified Guan/Ge/Longquan set measuring natural-surface **Ra**, spectral iridescence, and gloss across fresh/heirloom/excavated states was found. Layered glass corrosion can be an iridescence mechanism in glass generally, but the sources here do not establish it for Met 48503. Do not add rainbow interference as a mandatory archaeological Guan feature, or equate a fresh crack's angle-dependent silver-white glint with a weathering interference film.

## 4. Rendering decisions and the 130 µm band

The following are **I: physically motivated modelling choices**, not measurements of Song Guan.

| Control | Fresh, before staining | Heirloom / 传世 | Excavated / 出土 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fired glaze/body | Establish thick glaze, dark body, bubbles/crystals and colour first | Retain original fired structure | Retain original fired structure; allow selective dissolution later |
| Crack optics | Nonpigmented fracture interfaces; visibility varies with lighting, filling and orientation | Mix old stained cracks and later pale cracks if the chosen history warrants it | Preserve that chronology but allow soil deposits and crack-adjacent attack |
| Deposit | Zero in the explicitly unstained preset | Independent deliberate colouring and/or use/handling contamination | Independent soil/water chemistry, deposition and coverage; not necessarily black |
| Altered wall region | Zero for a chemically fresh assumption | Optional, history-dependent | Optional heterogeneous porous/altered region; do not force uniform width |
| Surface state | Firing-dependent smooth/satin appearance | Local scratches, wear and contamination as needed | Variable pits, loss of gloss, residual shiny islands and possible spalling |

A practical decomposition is **fracture optics + deposit absorption + porous-zone scattering + original glaze volume + surface topography**. Do not bake all five into one opaque yellow curve. A nonpigmented fracture can have a bright/glinting or weak/invisible appearance depending on geometry and index contrast; “white/silvery” describes an optical appearance, not proof of silver or a white mineral. These are conditional optical inferences; no fresh Guan crack reflectance spectrum was found.

For the reference, match the brief's **130 µm apparent band** as an image-space/world-scale target after the full lighting and transport setup. Keep actual slit aperture and any halo width separately editable. **Do not set each wall's alteration depth to 65 µm**: that assumes a symmetric section, direct physical-to-image mapping and no scattering contribution, none of which is measured. A pale-yellow band brighter than its surroundings may combine weak absorption and extra scattering from pores/microcracks/crystals; colour alone cannot distinguish that from other crack-associated optical effects. The sources establish plausibility, not the sherd's diagnosis.

A discriminating physical study would combine calibrated reflected/transmitted microscopy with a cross-section normal to a crack, SEM-BSE/EDS profiles, hydration-sensitive analysis, and micro-Raman mineral identification. That would test whether the bright band coincides with a porous altered region and where Fe/C is located. Without such evidence, retain alternative optical explanations as parameters rather than calling the 130 µm width a measured corrosion layer.

## 5. Explicit evidence gaps and limitations

- No published fresh-versus-heirloom-versus-excavated time series for the same Guan composition and firing was located.
- No physical crack aperture, hydration profile, Raman residue analysis or natural-surface profilometry of **Met 48503** was found. Its excavation/burial history is not established by the visible museum record.
- No measured fresh Guan crack colour/spectrum or universal white/silver appearance; no controlled observation that a particular secondary crack stays pale for a specified number of years.
- No general proof that all heirloom 金丝铁线 was deliberately dyed, or all coloured excavated Guan crackle was acquired in soil. Specific specimen findings must remain specific.
- No verified Song Guan tea/iron-solution recipe; later museum descriptions and modern reconstruction are not Song production records.
- No universal age-to-alteration thickness law. The glass analogues have different Al/Na/Ca content and exposure conditions; deposit thickness, depleted depth and dissolved thickness are different quantities.
- No natural-surface Ra values or controlled ageing loss in GU for the requested Guan/Ge states; no material-specific quantitative definition of 酥油光 or verified Guan iridescent-film thickness.
- Full original PDF/image downloads were blocked. Some evidence is publisher abstracts or search-indexed PDF text rather than independently viewable full pages; each restricted source is labelled. Local `.web.txt` archives are evidence records, not substitute originals.
