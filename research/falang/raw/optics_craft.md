## Question 2 — enamel glass optics

**O1.** Wendy Meulebroeck, Hilde Wouters, Karin Nys and Hugo Thienpont, “Authenticity screening of stained glass windows using optical spectroscopy,” *Scientific Reports* **6**, 37726 (2016), DOI [10.1038/srep37726](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep37726). [Full text opened](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5121600/).

- [M] Fig. 2 plots spectral measurements of three blue stained-glass groups; cobalt absorption is visible. Its first absorption band is 534.2 ± 1.6 nm in these specimens. These are European window glasses, explicitly **substitutes**, not Chinese enamels.
- [D] O1 compares calco-potassic glass (526.5 ± 1.5 nm) with soda-based glass (535 ± 2 nm), citing Ceglia et al. The host matrix changes band positions; these literature values were not independently checked in the original Ceglia source.
- [M] In ten red flashed panes, Fig. 5 resolves a copper-nanoparticle SPR peak at 563.1 ± 0.8 nm and a broader contribution near 430 nm.
- [I] Cu(0) nanoparticle red cannot stand in for a Cu(II) turquoise absorption coefficient: they are different species. Band positions alone do not determine attenuation per mm. No absolute lead-potash-enamel coefficient was extracted.
- [I] No verified source in this work package gives historical cloisonné refractive index, PbO-to-index calibration, polishing roughness, gloss units, or a renderer microfacet roughness. These values are **not found**, not zero.
