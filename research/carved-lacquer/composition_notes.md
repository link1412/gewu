# Composition / stratigraphy research notes

Researched 2026-10-29. Scope: item 2. **No original PDFs or image binaries were downloaded**; only some publisher content could be read or indexed. The following is research metadata, not a claim that the linked originals are present locally.

## Best directly verified numerical result: carved imperial screen

Václav Pitthard, Sabine Stanek, Martina Griesser, Christiane Jordan, Silvia Miklin-Kniefacz & Richard Miklin, “The technical investigation of an eighteenth-century Chinese imperial carved lacquer screen and its role in developing an appropriate conservation treatment,” *Studies in Conservation* 61:sup3 (2016), 97–108, DOI [10.1080/00393630.2016.1227117](https://doi.org/10.1080/00393630.2016.1227117). [Publisher full text](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00393630.2016.1227117); [PDF endpoint](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/00393630.2016.1227117).

In “Results and discussion / Method of manufacture and stratigraphy of lacquers,” the authors report **approximately 4 mm total lacquer thickness and around 50 layers**, from **optical microscopy and SEM** (**measured/observed, primary**). The front sequence is yellow, then green, then red, over a grey ground. This is polychrome carved lacquer, not a pure tihong or tixi specimen. A division 4 mm / 50 gives **approximately 80 µm average per application** (**our inference**, not a measured uniform layer thickness).

Figure 4 shows the front layer structure. Figure 5 gives Hirox ×50 and SEM BSE ×80 views from the right base. Figure 7 shows a ×200 cross-section of the back frame: ground / black lacquer / second ground / **two red layers** / yellow decoration (**observed count**, not front relief strata). Pigments: cinnabar red; orpiment yellow; carbon black; green attributed to orpiment plus indigo. Transparent surface coatings include restoration pine resin and shellac: avoid treating their appearance as clear urushi.

## Verified explicit modern red recipe (not traditional carved lacquer)

“The Influence of Natural Light Aging on the Surface Properties of Colored Chinese Lacquer Coatings,” *Coatings* **15**(10) (2025), 1229, [DOI 10.3390/coatings15101229](https://doi.org/10.3390/coatings15101229); [publisher](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-6412/15/10/1229); [PDF endpoint](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-6412/15/10/1229/pdf).

Methods state that urushiol-based resins from the Xi’an Chinese Lacquer Research Institute were mixed at a **10 wt% pigment-to-lacquer ratio**; the red pigment was cinnabar, **≥99% purity** (**prepared experimental recipe, primary**, not an analysis of a historical object). Preserve the denominator: the wording suggests pigment/lacquer = 0.10; if literally so, pigment/total wet mixture = **9.09 wt%** (**inference**). Do not silently call it 10% of the final cured film. There is no measured pigment volume fraction here.

## Historical recipe lead, insufficient for carved-lacquer calibration

“宁绍地区传统嫁妆的装饰风格,” [People’s Daily art channel, 2018-03-12](https://art.people.com.cn/n1/2018/0312/c226026-29861853.html), reports a **thick-paste recipe of red transparent polishing lacquer 30%, guang oil 20%, colourant 50%**. **Secondary claim** (“根据漆工资料记载”); percentage basis is unstated and the context is Ningbo/Shaoxing red-and-gold carved wood decoration, not a directly documented tihong cross-section. It cannot establish 50 wt% cinnabar in carved lacquer: “colourant” is not automatically pure cinnabar, and the source has not specified mass versus volume.

No trustworthy numeric **traditional cinnabar:urushi ratio for historical tihong** was verified in this search. Historical recipes vary by craft and oil content. A source giving lacquer:tung oil = 1:1 does not answer the pigment fraction.

## Tixi primary papers: useful, but thickness numbers not yet primary-verified

Xinying Hao et al., “Use of THM-PY-GC/MS technique to characterize complex, multilayered Chinese lacquer,” *Journal of Analytical and Applied Pyrolysis* **140** (2019), 339–348, [DOI 10.1016/j.jaap.2019.04.011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaap.2019.04.011), [publisher](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0165237018311689).

The publisher abstract directly reports a Song-dynasty Zhejiang tixi microsample with **one wood layer, one lacquer-ash layer, one ground-lacquer layer and four pigmented lacquer-film layers** (**observed cross-section count, primary**). Colourants were soot, orpiment and cinnabar. Organic ingredients included **laccol**, heat-bodied tung oil, and tannins in some layers. Thus historical Chinese tixi is not guaranteed to have an urushiol-only binder. No thickness or pigment particle dimensions were retrievable from the accessible abstract. Full-text section “Surface and cross-section morphology analysis” is the priority for acquisition.

Hongqiong Zhang, Hao Wu, Yang Zhao, Kun Zhang & Jingren Dong, “Multi-Analytical Characterization of the Materials and Manufacturing Technology of a Southern Song Dynasty Tixi Lacquer Plate from the Nanhai No. 1 Shipwreck,” *Coatings* **16**(9) (2026), 1034, published 31 August 2026, **14 pages**, [DOI 10.3390/coatings16091034](https://doi.org/10.3390/coatings16091034), [publisher](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-6412/16/9/1034), [review reports](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-6412/16/9/1034/review_report). Metadata and author list verified from the [publisher issue listing](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-6412/16/9?page=2).

The [secondary Chinese abstract](https://www.ebiotrade.com/newsf/2026-9/20260901104106580.htm) says **nine lacquer strata totalling 387.3 µm, plus a heterogeneous ground = ten visually distinct strata**, with alternating dark, red, and yellow layers (**secondary numerical claim pending original**). This translation contains obvious mistranslations (e.g. plant names and arsenic pigment names), so do not use its chemistry uncritically. The primary review reports confirm a layer-thickness table, Figure 2 cross-sectional microscopy, and an approximately **10 × 5 mm stratified detached fragment** (author response). Reviewers requested uncertainties; authors explain that the archived record does not support invented statistical uncertainties. Main text/table remain inaccessible.

Potential direct PDF path (unverified asset): `https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/coatings/coatings-16-01034/article_deploy/coatings-16-01034.pdf`.

## Cross-section micrograph leads, no accepted new dimensions

Na Wang et al., “Analytical investigation into materials and technique: Carved lacquer decorated panel from Fuwangge in the Forbidden City of Qianlong Period, Qing Dynasty,” *Journal of Archaeological Science: Reports* **17** (2018), 529–537, [DOI 10.1016/j.jasrep.2017.12.023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jasrep.2017.12.023), [publisher](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352409X17306879). Primary abstract says confocal microscopy measured the cross-section, and identifies **three main construction regions**: mortar, carved yellow lacquer, carved red lacquer (**observed**). Red is cinnabar and yellow orpiment; lacquer and boiled tung oil are binders. No numeric thickness from the accessible publisher abstract.

The Palace Museum’s institutional article [PDF](https://www.dpm.org.cn/Uploads/File/2018/12/11/u5c0f2af6cbe7c.pdf), printed p. **015**, Figure **24**, reproduces a cross-section of this carved red lacquer with a **2 mm scale bar** and component table. This is a useful downloadable micrograph target; original PDF not saved. Organic table: red = Chinese lacquer, boiled tung oil, beeswax; yellow = lacquer and boiled tung oil; ground = boiled tung oil and porcine blood.

唐慧娟, “山西大同地区出土漆器工艺浅析,” *文物世界* **2020**(3), printed pp. **65 onward**, [Shanxi University PDF](https://ygx.sxu.edu.cn/db/%E6%9C%9F%E5%88%8A/wwsj/wwsj2020/2003pdf/200320.pdf). Section III “金代墓出土剔犀漆器工艺,” starts printed p. **66**, with tixi cross-sections Figures **5–6** on the following page. These need original acquisition. Important: the **100 µm ground, 25 µm base lacquer and 19.5 µm red layer** appearing on p.66 belong to the **Northern Wei Sima Jinlong painted screen** section, **not tixi**; do not misattribute them.

## Pigment size: gap and misleading near-match

“Preparation and Properties of Composite Coatings Fabricated from Carved Lacquer Waste and Waterborne Acrylic Resin,” *Coatings* **15**(10) (2025), 1230, [DOI 10.3390/coatings15101230](https://doi.org/10.3390/coatings15101230), [publisher](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-6412/15/10/1230), §2.1 and Figure 1. Carved red lacquer waste supplied by Yangzhou Lacquerware Factory was **ground and sieved through 200 mesh**. Figure 1c describes irregular particles “a few micrometers to several tens of micrometers.” **These are milled composite lacquer-waste particles, not securely isolated cinnabar grains in an original carved-lacquer cross-section.** The >50% residue at 800°C also cannot safely be converted into original HgS wt% because cinnabar is volatile/chemically transforms on heating. Do not use these values as the requested historical cinnabar particle distribution.

No defensible cinnabar particle-size distribution specifically in carved-lacquer cross-sections, nor measured thin black/yellow marker-band thickness, was verified. The 1988 Getty *Urushi* technical study by Andreas Burmester contains many cross-section images and pigment identifications, a strong next acquisition target. Correct title/year for ISBN 0892360968: *Urushi: Proceedings of the Urushi Study Group, June 10–27, 1985, Tokyo* (Getty, **1988**). [Legacy PDF URL](https://www.getty.edu/publications/resources/virtuallibrary/0892360968.pdf) currently redirects to [Getty publication record](https://www.getty.edu/publications-reports/item/248V90).
