# Guan crackle: cross-section, network geometry, and scaled references

Research checked 29 October 2026. **The literature supports a visible subsurface fracture/deposit region wider than its surface mouth, but does not uniquely explain the Met sherd's 130 µm bright band.** The strongest new direct geometry evidence is C3; subsurface yellow deposits are documented in C4. No measured Guan lateral alteration-halo thickness or chipped-mouth width/depth was found.

The brief's measurements are inputs, not independently verified measurements: Met 48503 photograph approximately 32 px/mm; pale-yellow band approximately 130 µm and 17% brighter; target glaze thickness 0.7–1.5 mm. Numbers from different specimens must not be combined into a single measured Guan cross-section.

**Acquisition limitation:** the research report and provenance manifests are saved; **zero original paper PDFs or image rasters were successfully downloaded**. Shell networking is denied and browser saving was unavailable. Linked sources were read through web text/indexed original PDFs; raster inspection, native image dimensions and the ≥20 px/mm requirement remain unverified. See §3 and §6.

## Evidence conventions

- **M**: measured or directly observed by the cited source on its own specimens. Approximate image observations are identified separately from metrology.
- **Q**: quoted from another source, with the original reference identified where possible.
- **D**: arithmetic derived here from stated inputs; not a published specimen measurement.
- **U**: unresolved or unavailable; no number inferred from an uncalibrated illustration.
- **T**: theoretical/model result, explicitly distinguished from a specimen measurement.

Opening width, surface-mouth damage width, optical-band width, stained fracture depth, and lateral altered-glass thickness are different quantities. None should be substituted for another without evidence.

### N4. Drying alumina: measured angle spread and polygon sides

**Kelly A. Shorlin, John R. de Bruyn, Malcolm Graham, Stephen W. Morris**, “Development and geometry of isotropic and directional shrinkage-crack patterns,” *Physical Review E* **61**, 6950–6957, **2000**. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.61.6950); [primary preprint checked](https://arxiv.org/pdf/patt-sol/9911003), preprint pp.4–6/Figs.9–11.

**M**: mean joining angle about **90°**. Untreated substrate: angular SD about **15°**, no thickness trend. Reduced friction: SD about **5° at 2.5 mm**, increasing to **20° at 0.7 mm**. No significant **120°** peak. Geometric-side histogram peaks at **4**, essentially unchanged over **0.13–4.12 mm** layer depths. Exact bin proportions are graphical, not transcribed here. Spacing grows approximately linearly with thickness; lower friction increases it. Curved arches, ladder rungs and late corner-cutting cracks occur, without a measured tortuosity distribution. This is **alumina/water desiccation**, not ceramic glaze cooling.

### N6. Repeated mud cracking: angles, areas and incomplete connections

**Lucas Goehring, Rebecca Conroy, Asad Akhter, William J. Clegg, Alexander F. Routh**, “Evolution of mud-crack patterns during repeated drying cycles,” *Soft Matter* **6**, 3562–3567, **2010**. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1039/B922206E); [institutional PDF](https://irep.ntu.ac.uk/id/eprint/28373/1/5915_Goehring.pdf), pp.3564–3566/Figs.4–5.

- **M**: four **3.1±0.3 mm** bentonite layers, **25 cycles**, **100–200 angles/sample**. Initially near-90° junctions evolve toward **120°**, relaxation about **4 generations**.
- **M**: mean cell area increases **20% in first 5 cycles**; relative area spread SD/mean changes at most **10% over 25 cycles**. No fitted probability law given.
- **M**: below about **1.5 mm**, cracks mostly stop before meeting others. Random-line spacing is **10–15% smaller** than √(mean cell area), because it includes unconnected cracks.
- **M**: changed opening-sequence probability **0.28±0.04** between first two cycles. The accompanying T-fraction decay model is **T**, not measured junction fractions.

Rewetting/healing permits path rearrangement; this cannot be equated with a second irreversible craze generation in fired glaze.

### N7. Two spatial scales need not mean identical repeated networks

**Xiaolei Ma, Janna Lowensohn, Justin C. Burton**, “Universal Scaling of Polygonal Desiccation Crack Patterns,” *Physical Review E* **99**, 012802, **2019**. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.99.012802); [primary manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/1807.06126v2), §§III.1,III.3,III.6/Figs.2–4,8.

**M + fitted scaling**: particle-film polygon area follows **Aₚ = αh^(4/3)** in their experiments. Cornstarch/water produces primary capillary-shrinkage polygons, then secondary polygons from particle deswelling; secondary area saturates for thick films. This is an analogue, **not a Guan cell-area distribution or glaze thickness law**.

**Ankush Kumar, Rajashekhar Pujar, Nikita Gupta, Sujata Tarafdar, Giridhar U. Kulkarni**, “Stress modulation in desiccating crack networks for producing effective templates for patterning metal network based transparent conductors,” *Applied Physics Letters* **111**, 013502, **2017**. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.4991534); [author text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/318244825_Stress_modulation_in_desiccating_crack_networks_for_producing_effective_templates_for_patterning_metal_network_based_transparent_conductors), manuscript pp.3,5/Figs.1–2.

**M**: **1.7 µm** acrylic-resin particle film, initial cracks about **700 nm**; **20** isopropanol wet/dry cycles extend/nucleate cracks. Derived conductive backbone fraction rises **63%→82%**. Its complement includes dangling/isolated structures; it is **not** a dead-end-crack fraction. Repeated exposure can increase connectivity rather than yield mud-like angle relaxation.

### N8. Measured cell-area distributions and side counts in starch

**Yuri Akiba, Jun Magome, Hiroshi Kobayashi, Hiroyuki Shima**, “Morphometric analysis of polygonal cracking patterns in desiccated starch slurries,” *Physical Review E* **96**, 023003, **2017**. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.96.023003); [primary preprint checked](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.07768), §III.C, preprint pp.5–7/Eq.2/Figs.6–8.

**M + empirical fits**: secondary-crack cell areas follow **P(S)=c exp(−S/β²)**. For layer thickness increasing **2→11 mm**, fitted β rises **4.4±0.2→7.4±0.3 mm** in potato starch; **3.6±0.1→4.5±0.2 mm** in corn starch. Five samples per thickness; several tens/hundreds of cells per sample. This is a measured analogue distribution, not a glaze law.

**M**: vertex-count histogram peaks at **5**, followed by 4/6; peak stays at 5 over **2,5,8,11 mm** layers. Counts include inward kinks, so this is not the same side definition as N3. Isolated/dead-end cracks were **removed before analysis** (§II.B); these statistics cannot establish their frequency. Figure bins are not tabulated as exact percentages.

### N9. Crack-derived thin-film networks: tortuosity and dead-end length fraction

**Tirumala Rao Dumpala, S. Kiruthika, Siddhi Vinayak Pandey, Junyang Zhang, Solleti Goutham, Marcos Vinicius Surmani Martins, Harry Whittingham, Boya Radha, Ashok Keerthi**, “Scalable Crackle-Lithography for Multifunctional Enclosed Plastic Nanofluidic Devices,” *Advanced Science*, e76980, **2026**. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.76980); [primary PDF checked](https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1002/advs.76980), Results/p.3, citing Figs.S8–S10.

**M**, optical images of crack-derived PETG nanochannel networks:

- Apparent tortuosity, **transport-path length / straight-line distance: 1.3±0.2**.
- Dead-end fraction: **0.3±0.2%**, defined as endpoint-to-nearest-junction terminal-branch **length / total skeleton length**.
- Largest connected component: **99.5±0.2% of network area**.

These are authored main-text statistics; supplementary plots were not independently inspected. The checked passage does not specify the ± convention. Tortuosity concerns network transport paths, **not individual between-junction segment straightness**. Length fraction is not a segment-count or endpoint fraction. This lithographic thin-film analogue does not establish Guan values.

For microscope figures, px/mm must be computed from **native pixels spanning the printed scale bar**, not from DPI or added upsampling. Whole-object catalogue dimensions provide only nominal projected scale; they do not locally calibrate curved or oblique surfaces. Acquisition manifests are [papers/sources.tsv](papers/sources.tsv) and [images/downloads.tsv](images/downloads.tsv).

## 4. Explaining the 130 µm band

At the brief's 32 px/mm, 130 µm spans **4.16 pixels**, while a 10 µm opening spans **0.32 pixel** (**D**). Thus the photograph cannot resolve the opening directly. The measured band includes the image-formation response and potentially subsurface structure. The calculation does not identify which physical mechanism dominates.

| Candidate explanation | Evidence | What remains unmeasured |
|---|---|---|
| Visible inclined/shallow fracture face | C1 and N2; shallow fractures associated with cloudiness | Visible depth, scattering strength and projected width for Met 48503 |
| Internally wider crack channel / deposits | C3 section metrology; C4 subsurface yellow material | Corresponding geometry/chemistry of the Met sherd |
| Chipped surface mouth | C1 qualitative chips | Width/depth; cannot justify a 130 µm trench |
| Lateral hydrated/leached glass halo | Crack-adjacent marine corrosion in C5 | No measured Guan/Ge halo thickness; no identified 130 µm altered zone |
| Optical/imaging spread | Opening subpixel at the brief's scale | Lens/defocus/scattering response and original pixel profile |

**Geometrical feasibility, not identification:** for a straight fracture observed normally, with its cross-section perpendicular to its surface trace, projected lateral span is **w = d tan θ**, where θ is tilt from the normal and d is the optically visible depth. To span 130 µm requires:

| Assumed θ | Required visible depth d (**D**) |
|---:|---:|
| 10° (brief's assumption; not independently verified) | 737 µm |
| 30° | 225 µm |
| 45° | 130 µm |
| 60° | 75 µm |

Those are possible geometrical scales within thick glaze; they are **not measurements of Met 48503**. For a fully visible 0.7–1.5 mm plane tilted 10–30°, the span would range **123–866 µm** (**D**). Neither full-depth visibility nor uniform scattering follows from inclination alone. OCT image brightness is also not a calibrated visible-light photograph.

**Conclusion:** an optically broad fracture/deposit region is supported better than treating 130 µm as a measured physical mouth width. Existing evidence does not select a unique mechanism or apportion its contribution. The brief's **+17%** is photographic contrast, not a calibrated reflectance measurement; no matching published bright-band profile was found. A rendering may keep aperture, subsurface fracture visibility and deposited colour as separate quantities, but their settings would remain a hypothesis for this object.
