# Brief: what a Guan crackle line is, in cross-section, and how crackle networks are shaped

For a film project we render Southern Song Guan glaze (官窑, thick bubbly celadon over a dark body, glaze about 0.7-1.5 mm) and need measured facts, not opinions.

In a museum photo (Met 48503, 32 px/mm) the crack lines are soft pale-yellow bands about 130 um wide, about 17% brighter than the glaze, while the crack opening itself is reported at about 10 um. We want to know why the band is that wide. Already have: Yang et al. 2012 (InTech OCT chapter, cracks inclined 10-30 deg with "chips" at the opening), Wu et al. 2025 (Laohudong golden thread / iron wire phases), the Palace Museum 2016 confocal note (crack widths ~10-14 um), Lahlil et al. 2013 (cell spacing 1.7-14.1 mm).

1. Cross-sections of crackle cracks in Guan, Ge, Longquan black-body or other thick celadon/stoneware glazes (SEM, thin section, OCT, confocal, micro-CT): inclination of the crack plane from the surface normal, crack opening, chipping or spalling at the crack mouth (its width and depth), whether cracks branch, step or run en echelon through the glaze layers, and how deep the stain reaches; any staining or weathering (hydrated/leached layer) that extends into the glass beside the crack, with its thickness.
2. Measured statistics of crack networks in glazes (crackle, crazing) or, failing that, in other thin brittle films (paint craquelure, drying films, mud cracks): fractions of T, Y and X junctions, junction angles, segment straightness or tortuosity, cell area distribution and number of sides, short or partial cracks. Include how the pattern depends on glaze thickness, layering, or a second (delayed) crazing.
3. Close-up photos of Guan or Ge crackle lines with a known scale, at least about 20 px/mm, especially pale yellow lines; and any cross-section images. Reference only, any licence; save them under `images/` with a sources list.

Deliverable: `report.md` in this directory (write it as you go): per item, the source (authors, title, venue, year, DOI or URL, page or figure), the numbers, and whether each number was measured in that source or quoted from elsewhere. Downloaded papers and images in their own folders. Say plainly what you could not find.
